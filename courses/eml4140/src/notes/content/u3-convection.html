Part I

The thermal layer

1Two layers on a heated plate

A stream at \(T_\infty\) flows along a plate held at \(T_s\). Fluid that has touched the plate long enough to warm up forms a thin layer. Heat diffuses out through it while the stream sweeps it along, so it grows as \(\sqrt x\), like the velocity layer.

Derivation

In a moving fluid the heat equation gains a material derivative: \(DT/Dt = \alpha\nabla^2T\). In a thin layer only conduction across it counts, so

\[ u\frac{\partial T}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial T}{\partial y} = \alpha\frac{\partial^2 T}{\partial y^2}. \]

With the velocity layer's similarity variable \(\eta = y\sqrt{U/\nu x}\), \(u/U = f'(\eta)\) and \(\theta = (T - T_s)/(T_\infty - T_s)\), it becomes

\[ \theta'' + \frac{\text{Pr}}{2}\,f\,\theta' = 0, \qquad \theta(0) = 0, \quad \theta(\infty) = 1, \qquad \text{Pr} = \frac{\nu}{\alpha}. \]

Only Pr enters. At Pr = 1 the equation is the Blasius equation for \(f'\), so the two layers match. Above 1, momentum spreads faster than heat and the thermal layer is the thinner one, close to \(\delta_t/\delta = \text{Pr}^{-1/3}\).

Worked example · air over a warm plate

Air has Pr = 0.71. Solving the equation numerically gives \(\theta = 0.99\) at \(\eta = 5.60\), against 4.91 for the velocity layer, so

\[ \frac{\delta_t}{\delta} = \frac{5.60}{4.91} = 1.14, \qquad \text{Pr}^{-1/3} = 1.12. \]

The wall gradient is \(\theta'(0) = 0.294\), within 1% of \(0.332(0.71)^{1/3} = 0.296\).

Common mistake

Taking the thermal layer to be the velocity layer. In water, Pr is about 6 and the thermal layer is half as thick; in an oil, a tenth or less; in a liquid metal, several times thicker.

2Heat leaving the wall

The fluid at the wall does not move, so heat leaves the plate by conduction into it. The flow's job is to keep the temperature gradient there steep.

Derivation

At the wall \(q'' = -k\,\partial T/\partial y\). With \(\partial\eta/\partial y = \sqrt{U/\nu x}\),

\[ q'' = k\,(T_s - T_\infty)\,\theta'(0)\sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}}, \qquad \text{Nu}_x = \frac{q''x}{k\,(T_s - T_\infty)} = \theta'(0)\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}. \]

For Pr above about 0.6, \(\theta'(0) \approx 0.332\,\text{Pr}^{1/3}\). For a liquid metal, \(\theta'(0) \approx 0.564\,\text{Pr}^{1/2}\). The flux falls as \(1/\sqrt x\): the layer thickens and the gradient flattens.

Worked example · water along a plate

Water at 0.5 m/s with \(k = 0.613\) W/m·K, \(\nu = 8.57\times10^{-7}\) m²/s and Pr = 5.83, over a plate 40 K warmer. The exact solution gives \(\theta'(0) = 0.607\); the rule gives 0.598. At \(x = 0.3\) m, \(\text{Re}_x = 1.75\times10^5\) and

\[ q'' = 0.613(40)(0.607)\sqrt{\frac{0.5}{8.57\times10^{-7}(0.3)}} = 20.8\ \text{kW/m}^2. \]
Key idea

\(h = q''/(T_s - T_\infty)\) is not a property of the fluid. It depends on how thin the thermal layer is, so on the speed, the distance from the leading edge and Pr.
