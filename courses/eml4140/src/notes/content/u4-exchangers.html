Part I

Two streams, and evaporation

1Heat exchangers

A heat exchanger passes heat from one stream to another through a wall. Its conductance \(UA\) sums the resistances in between.

Derivation

Energy balances on both streams over an element \(dA\) give \(C_h\,dT_h = -U(T_h - T_c)\,dA\) and \(\pm C_c\,dT_c = U(T_h - T_c)\,dA\), with \(C = \dot m c_p\). Integrated, \(q = UA\,\Delta T_\text{lm}\). Written with \(\text{NTU} = UA/C_\text{min}\) and \(C_r = C_\text{min}/C_\text{max}\), counterflow gives

\[ \varepsilon = \frac{1 - e^{-\text{NTU}(1 - C_r)}}{1 - C_r\,e^{-\text{NTU}(1 - C_r)}}, \qquad q = \varepsilon\,C_\text{min}(T_{h,i} - T_{c,i}), \]

and parallel flow \(\varepsilon = [1 - e^{-\text{NTU}(1 + C_r)}]/(1 + C_r)\). With equal streams, counterflow gives \(\varepsilon = \text{NTU}/(1 + \text{NTU})\).

Worked example · equal streams at NTU 2

Hot water at 90 °C and cold at 20 °C, both with \(C = 1000\) W/K, through \(UA = 2000\) W/K. In counterflow \(\varepsilon = 2/3\): the duty is 46.7 kW, the cold stream leaves at 66.7 °C and the hot at 43.3 °C. In parallel flow \(\varepsilon = 0.491\) and the duty is 34.4 kW.

Common mistake

Thinking the cold stream can never leave hotter than the hot stream leaves. In counterflow the cold outlet faces the hot inlet, so it can: 66.7 °C against 43.3 °C above. Only parallel flow keeps \(T_{c,o} < T_{h,o}\).

2Evaporation and the wet bulb

Vapour carried off a wet surface obeys the same equations as heat: Fick's law for Fourier's, concentration for temperature.

Derivation

The vapour flux is \(\dot m'' = h_m(\rho_{v,s} - \rho_{v,\infty})\), and each kilogram carries \(h_{fg}\). The Chilton–Colburn analogy gives \(h/h_m = \rho c_p\,\text{Le}^{2/3}\), with \(\text{Le} = \alpha/D_{AB}\). In steady state the air's convection pays for the evaporation:

\[ T_\infty - T_w = \frac{h_{fg}}{\rho c_p\,\text{Le}^{2/3}}\left[\rho_{v,\text{sat}}(T_w) - \phi\,\rho_{v,\text{sat}}(T_\infty)\right]. \]

\(h\) cancels: the wet-bulb temperature does not depend on the air speed.

Worked example · air at 30 °C and 50%

Air at 30 °C holds 15.2 g/m³ of vapour at 50%. Solving the balance, the wick settles at 21.5 °C, 8.5 K below the air, where \(\rho_{v,\text{sat}} = 18.9\) g/m³. The dew point is 18.4 °C.

Common mistake

Thinking a wet surface settles at the air temperature, or at the dew point. It settles between them, where convection in balances latent heat out. Only in saturated air do all three meet.
