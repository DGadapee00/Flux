# Provenance

EML 3100 Thermodynamics lives in this folder. There is no earlier repo to snapshot. The course is
written here, unit by unit. Units 1 and 2 are in.

## Textbooks (DECISIONS.md, D13)

| Book | Licence | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Claire Yu Yan, *Introduction to Engineering Thermodynamics*, BCcampus, 2022 (pressbooks.bccampus.ca/thermo1) | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | the engineering units, cited by section |
| Samuel J. Ling, Jeff Sanny and William Moebs, *University Physics Volume 2*, OpenStax, 2016, Ch 1–4 | CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 | the physics background, cited by section |

Nothing is copied from either book: no prose, figures, tables, worked examples or problems. Flux
writes its own text. The books appear only as citations: section numbers and titles, all in one
map, `src/data/textbook.js`. Changing books changes that file. Yan's section numbers are the
BCcampus edition's; the LibreTexts copy numbers some sections one higher.

Topics past Yan's Chapter 6 (the Rankine and Brayton cycles, vapour-compression refrigeration)
are cited by topic only, until a source is chosen.

The course pack sets which topics are covered and in what order (D11). Nothing from the pack ships.

## Property data

Every property comes from `@flux/subjects/thermo`: IAPWS-IF97 for water, NASA polynomials (GRI-Mech
3.0 and NASA Glenn) for air. Its sources are in `packages/subjects/src/thermo/PROVENANCE.md`. The
property tables the problems interpolate in are generated by that library. No textbook table is
copied.

## What course material is here

Unit 1 is pure substances and ideal gases (Yan Ch 2–3). The lab is a P–v and T–v diagram of water
(`phase`) with the saturation dome and three paths: an isobar, a rigid tank and an isotherm. The
problems cover phases, quality, table interpolation, the ideal-gas law in SI and English units,
and the compressibility factor of steam.

Unit 2 is the first law for closed systems (Yan Ch 4). The lab is a piston–cylinder (`piston`)
with air at constant pressure, constant temperature, or polytropic, and steam at constant
pressure. The problems cover boundary work in SI and English units, rigid tanks of steam, steam at
constant pressure, and air with temperature-dependent specific heats.

All numbers in the problems are generated. Worked cases use numbers computed for this bank and
cite the section their method comes from. No textbook example or problem number is copied or
invented.

## Notes

`src/notes/content/` holds one file per unit, written for Flux. They cite Yan and OpenStax by
