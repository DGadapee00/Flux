Part I

Build the model

1Supports and rigid-body equilibrium

A rigid body must satisfy force balance and moment balance. In a plane these are \(\sum F_x=0\), \(\sum F_y=0\), and \(\sum M_O=0\). Choose a moment point that eliminates inconvenient unknown forces. A pin supplies two force components. A smooth roller supplies one normal force. A fixed wall also supplies a couple.

For a point load P at a on a simply supported span L, moments about A give \(B_yL=Pa\). Hence \(B_y=Pa/L\), then \(A_y=P-B_y\). Equal reactions occur only at midspan. A negative roller reaction means an unrestrained contact would lift off.

A distributed load is replaced by equal total force and equal first moment: \(W=\int w(x)\,dx\), \(\bar x=\int xw(x)\,dx/W\). A uniform load has resultant wL at L/2. A triangular load increasing from zero has resultant wL/2 at 2L/3. These replacements preserve external reactions, but not internal forces at an arbitrary cut.

Spatial rigid-body equilibrium

In three dimensions, zero acceleration requires six scalar balances: three force components and three moment components. A ball-and-socket support restrains translation and supplies three forces, but no moment. A smooth bearing supplies forces perpendicular to its shaft; axial thrust requires a thrust-bearing constraint. A cable supplies one force along its known direction. Model the restraint before choosing the unknowns.

Write every reaction with its point of application and unit direction. Its moment column is \(\mathbf r\times\hat{\mathbf u}\). A reaction couple enters the moment equations directly. These columns form a six-row equilibrium matrix. Six reaction unknowns are sufficient only when those columns are independent. A support layout can leave rotation unrestrained even when the count looks correct.

Worked example · spatial support check

A rigid plate has a socket at A(0,0,0) m, a smooth x-directed bearing at B(2,0,0), and a vertical cable at C(0,1,0). A 100 N weight acts at (0.8,0.4,0). The bearing supplies only By and Bz. Moments about x give T − 40 = 0. Moments about y give −2Bz + 80 = 0. Thus T = 40 N and Bz = 40 N. Moments about z give 2By = 0, hence By = 0. Force balance gives Ax = Ay = 0 and Az = 20 N. The six equations close without inventing a reaction moment.

This supplementary spatial example is a free-body calculation. The reaction lab shows planar beams only.

Common mistake

A pin cannot supply a reaction couple. A fixed support must supply one when the applied loads have a net moment.

Worked example · Offset load

For L = 10 ft, P = 600 lb and a = 4 ft, \(B_y=600(4)/10=240\) lb and \(A_y=360\) lb. Check about B: \(360(10)=600(6)\).

Worked example · Triangular loading

On a 6 ft span, a triangular load rises from zero to 90 lb/ft. Its area is 270 lb and its centroid is 4 ft from A. Thus \(B_y=180\) lb and \(A_y=90\) lb. The larger reaction lies near the deeper part of the loading.

Worked example · Fixed wall

An 8 ft cantilever carries 250 lb at its tip and 50 lb/ft throughout. Vertical balance gives \(A_y=250+400=650\) lb. Moments give \(M_A=250(8)+400(4)=3600\) lb·ft counterclockwise.

What the model leaves out

Rigid-body equilibrium cannot predict support deflection or resolve redundant reactions without deformation information.

2Trusses: joints, sections and stability

A pin-connected truss member has two end forces and no other load. Its moment balance forces the two forces to be collinear. Force balance makes them equal and opposite. Each member therefore carries axial force. Assume tension initially; a negative solution means compression.

At an unloaded joint with two noncollinear members, each is zero by component balance. With three members, two collinear, the noncollinear member is zero. Applied loads or support reactions can invalidate either shortcut. A zero-force member for one load case may stabilise another.

The method of joints writes two equations at each joint. Start where at most two member forces remain unknown. The method of sections cuts the truss and balances one retained part. Choose a moment centre where unwanted cut-force lines intersect. Parallel cut forces cannot be eliminated by a finite intersection point; use a force equation instead.

Key idea · count is necessary, geometry decides

The count \(m+r=2j\) is necessary for a statically determinate plane truss. It is not sufficient. Collinear members, dependent restraints or an unbraced mechanism can leave the equation matrix singular. Check geometry and independence as well.

Common mistake

A negative member force does not mean the truss solver failed. It reverses the assumed tensile arrows and means compression.

Worked example · Three members

A triangular truss has 4 ft span, 3 ft rise and 1000 lb downward at the apex. Each reaction is 500 lb. With diagonal length \(\sqrt{13}\) ft, \(F_{AC}=-500\sqrt{13}/3=-600.925\) lb. Horizontal joint balance gives \(F_{AB}=333.333\) lb tension.

Worked example · Pratt section

The lab truss carries 900 lb at B and 300 lb at C. Whole-truss moments give \(A_y=700\) lb. Retain A, B and E. Moments about E give \(F_{BC}=700\) lb; about C give \(F_{EF}=-500\) lb. Vertical balance gives \(F_{EC}=\sqrt2(700-900)=-282.843\) lb.

What the model leaves out

Joints are frictionless pins, loads enter only at joints, and members are straight. Buckling, connection eccentricity and self-weight need additional models.

Part II

Use and check the model

3Frames and machines

A frame member can carry loads between its end pins, so it need not be a two-force member. First isolate the whole assembly to find external reactions. Then separate its members. Draw the interaction at an internal pin equal and opposite on the two free bodies. Those forces disappear only when the members are recombined.

A two-force member still helps inside a frame. Its end-force direction is fixed along the line between its pins. A three-force body in equilibrium has concurrent force lines, unless all three are parallel. Both statements follow by taking moments about the intersection of two lines.

For pliers, isolate one handle-and-jaw member. With perpendicular hand lever b and jaw lever a, moment balance gives \(N=Pb/a\). Vertical force balance then determines the pivot force. The pivot carries the jaw force and the applied hand force. It is not unloaded merely because its moment about itself vanishes.

Common mistake

Do not put an internal pin force on the whole-assembly free-body diagram. Put it on each separated member with opposite arrows.

Worked example · A-frame

The 600 lb load acts halfway along AB, at (1,1.5) ft. About A, \(4C_y=600(1)\), giving 150 lb. BC is a two-force member with slope −3/2, so \(C_x=-100\) lb and \(A_x=100\) lb. The apex pin carries \(\sqrt{100^2+150^2}=180.278\) lb.

Worked example · Pliers

Each handle sees 50 N at 3 in from the pin. The jaw acts 1 in away. Moments give 150 N on each jaw, then force balance gives 200 N at the pin. Equal and opposite jaw forces act on opposite faces of the bolt.

What the model leaves out

Ideal pins have no friction or moment resistance. Grip compliance, yielding and handle bending are excluded.

4A sign gantry and a scissor jack

The gantry lab models a triangular panel in plan view. Transverse sign force is \(W=pA\). Symmetry gives half to each support. Apex equilibrium gives diagonal compression \(C=W\ell/(2h)\), with \(\ell=\sqrt{(L/2)^2+h^2}\). Horizontal equilibrium gives chord tension \(WL/(4h)\). A section moment gives the same result directly: chord force times depth balances support force times half-span.

The jack has four equal arms of length a, height H and horizontal screw span x. Geometry gives \(x^2+H^2=4a^2\). At the upper joint, \(2C(H/2a)=W\), hence \(C=Wa/H\). At a side joint, \(Q=2C(x/2a)=Wx/H\). As the jack collapses, force grows even though its supported weight is unchanged.

Check the jack a second way by virtual work. Differentiating the geometry gives \(dH/dx=-x/H\). For an ideal mechanism, \(Q\,|dx|=W\,dH\), recovering \(Q=Wx/H\).

Common mistake

The screw tension is not the hand force. Thread pitch and friction are needed to relate axial force to input torque.

Worked example · Shallow gantry

A 30 in square sign has area \(0.762^2=0.580644\) m². At an assumed 600 Pa, \(W=348.3864\) N. For L = 4 m and h = 1 m, chord tension is 348.386 N and each reaction is 174.193 N. Halving depth doubles chord force.

Worked example · Raised jack

Use the nominal 1.5 US ton load as 3000 lbf, or 13344.665 N. For assumed 0.25 m arms at H = 0.30 m, x = 0.40 m. Each arm carries 11120.554 N compression and screw tension is 17792.886 N. A lower jack requires greater force.

What the model leaves out

Neither application computes strength, fatigue, buckling or a safe capacity. Manufacturer ratings are context for load scale, not validation of the assumed structure.
