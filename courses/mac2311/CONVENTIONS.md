# Calculus I and II conventions

- Follow 18.01SC's order, supplemented by OpenStax Calculus Volumes 1 and 2 (D28).
- The course ID stays `mac2311`; the header code is MAC 2311–2312.
- Canonical unit IDs are `c1`–`c6`. Old `u1`–`u4` identifiers always mean legacy links.
- Calculus I problem IDs are retained; Calculus II IDs use the `ii.` prefix.
- Seed generation uses the original template ID to preserve saved questions.
- Chapters without a prefix name Volume 1. `v2.2`, `v2.3`, `v2.5`, `v2.6`, `v2.7` name Volume 2.
- The geometric and numerical answers stay in their original units and conventions.
- Generated teaching numbers are verification cases, not newly verified printed answers.
- Notes use the shared shell's parts, legend, worked examples and model-limit boxes.
- `flux.mac2312.*` keys are retained for compatibility and old hub backups.

- Reworked units use finite designed function/configuration families. Retired templates remain in the legacy lookup so old review records and in-progress exams retain their exact questions.
- New symbolic worked cases check the published answer expression, including an equivalent rewrite and a named wrong expression. Definitions used by conceptual cases are identified separately from printed numeric answers.
- The prediction panel is an opt-in shared math-shell control. Its outcomes come from the actual lab recompute function.

- Rain-barrel time is minutes and flow is litres/minute. Nominal capacity uses US gallons through the shared exact conversion. The drawn water level is a capacity fraction; the schematic barrel is not a dimensional product drawing.
- Source corrections are labeled separately from common student mistakes and recorded in ERRATA.md. Generated notes examples are checked teaching cases, not claimed as printed source answers.

The Unit 4 cable uses metres and equal-height supports. Published span is distinguished from assumed sag. Surface area excludes end caps unless stated; radius is distance from the rotation axis. Arc length stays nonnegative, while an oriented slant integral can change sign under reversed limits.
