Functions and limits

A limit is the number the outputs approach. The value at the point can be missing. Every number below is recomputed.

01The limit of a function

A function assigns one output to each input in its domain. A limit asks what those outputs approach as the input approaches a number. The function need not have a value there.

Why

A polynomial is a sum of powers of \(x\). Each power has a limit, and the limit of a sum is the sum of the limits. Substituting the number therefore gives the limit.

\[ \lim_{x\to 2}(x^2+3x)=4+6=10. \]
Worked example · a polynomial at 2

Take \(x^2+3x\) as \(x\) approaches 2. The square approaches 4. The linear term approaches 6. The sum approaches 10. The value at 2 is also 10.

Common mistake

A student who will not substitute, because a limit never uses the point, reports that this limit does not exist. For a polynomial the value is the limit. OpenStax Calculus Volume 1 §2.2.

Key

For a polynomial, substitute. The limit equals the value.

This shortcut is for polynomials and other continuous functions. A hole, a jump, or a quotient that is zero over zero needs the two sides, which the next section computes.

02One-sided limits

The left-hand limit uses inputs below \(a\). The right-hand limit uses inputs above \(a\). The two-sided limit exists only when those two numbers are equal.

Why

Factor \(x^2-4=(x-2)(x+2)\). For \(x\neq 2\) the factor \(x-2\) cancels. Both sides then follow the line \(x+2\). The missing input is not part of the limit.

\[ \lim_{x\to 2}\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}=4. \]
Worked example · a hole at 2

Let \(f(x)=(x^2-4)/(x-2)\) for \(x\neq 2\). Then \(f(x)=x+2\). Both one-sided limits are 4, so the limit is 4. The function has no value at 2.

Common mistake

The limit is 4. A student who says the limit does not exist, because \(f(2)\) is undefined, has read the value instead of the two sides. OpenStax Calculus Volume 1 §2.3.

Worked example · sides that disagree

At \(a=0\) the left-hand limit is 0 and the right-hand limit is 1. The sides differ, so the two-sided limit does not exist. Their average, \(1/2\), is neither side.

Key

Both sides must give the same number. Otherwise the two-sided limit does not exist.

A jump and a sign change are the same test: compare the two sides. The picture in the lab marks each side. The missing point, when there is one, is an open circle.

03Limit laws

The limit of a sum is the sum of the limits. A constant factor comes out of a limit. Each piece must have a limit before the law applies.

Why

\(2x^2\) and \(3x\) each have a limit at 2. Add those two numbers. The factor 2 stays on the square. The factor 3 stays on \(x\).

\[ \lim_{x\to 2}(2x^2+3x)=2\cdot 4+3\cdot 2=14. \]
Worked example · a sum at 2

As \(x\) approaches 2, \(2x^2\) approaches 8 and \(3x\) approaches 6. The sum of the limits is 14. Sampling the function on both sides of 2 agrees.

Common mistake

A student who multiplies 3 by the square computes \(2\cdot 4+3\cdot 4=20\). The 3 multiplies \(x\), and the 2 multiplies \(x^2\). OpenStax Calculus Volume 1 §2.3.

Key

Split the sum. Scale each limit by its own factor.

The laws leave out a quotient whose denominator approaches 0. That case returns to the cancellation in the previous section, or it has no limit.

04Continuity

A function is continuous at \(a\) when the limit exists, the value \(f(a)\) exists, and the two are equal. A hole has a limit and no value, so it is not continuous there.

Why

Continuity is three checks. Drop any one and the name does not apply. The absolute value meets at its corner, so the value matches both sides. The two slopes do not match.

Worked example · a corner at 0

\(f(x)=|x|\). Both one-sided limits are 0, and \(f(0)=0\), so \(f\) is continuous at 0. The left difference quotient is \(-1\). The right one is \(1\). The derivative does not exist at 0.

Common mistake

Continuous does not mean differentiable. A student who marks \(|x|\) discontinuous at the corner, because of the sharp turn, has mixed up the two tests. OpenStax Calculus Volume 1 §2.4.

Key

The corner of \(|x|\) is continuous. It is not differentiable.

The hole at 2 from the earlier section has limit 4 and no value, so it fails continuity. Filling the hole with 4 would make it continuous. Filling it with any other number would not.

05Epsilon and delta

The limit is \(L\) when every \(\varepsilon>0\) has a \(\delta>0\) such that \(0<|x-a|<\delta\) forces \(|f(x)-L|<\varepsilon\). For a line through the origin, divide \(\varepsilon\) by the absolute value of the slope.

Why

\(|mx|<\varepsilon\) rearranges to \(|x|<\varepsilon/|m|\) when \(m\neq 0\). The largest such \(\delta\) is that quotient. Any smaller positive \(\delta\) also works. The definition asks for one that works.

\[ \delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{|m|}. \]
Worked example · a line of slope 2

Let \(f(x)=2x\) and \(\varepsilon=0.5\). Then \(\delta=0.5/2=0.25\). If \(|x|<0.25\), then \(|2x|<0.5\). A sample step has to be smaller than this \(\delta\) to stay inside the band.

Common mistake

A student who sets \(\delta=\varepsilon=0.5\) lets \(|x|=0.4\) into the window. Then \(|2x|=0.8\), which is past \(\varepsilon\). Divide by the slope. OpenStax Calculus Volume 1 §2.5.

Key

For \(f(x)=mx\) at 0, \(\delta=\varepsilon/|m|\).

The line in this section passes through the origin, so \(L=0\). A line \(mx+b\) uses the same \(\delta\), because the added constant cancels in \(|f(x)-f(a)|\).

06The squeeze theorem

If \(g(x)\le f(x)\le h(x)\) near \(a\), and \(g\) and \(h\) share a limit \(L\), then \(f\) has limit \(L\). The middle function is trapped between the two bounds.

Why

Sine stays between \(-1\) and \(1\). Multiply by \(x^2\), which is never negative. Then \(-x^2\le x^2\sin(1/x)\le x^2\). Both bounds approach 0 as \(x\) approaches 0, so the middle one does too.

Worked example · a sine squeezed to 0

Let \(f(0)=0\) and \(f(x)=x^2\sin(1/x)\) otherwise. Then \(-x^2\le f(x)\le x^2\) for \(x\neq 0\). Both bounds have limit 0. The limit of \(f\) is 0.

Common mistake

\(\sin(1/x)\) by itself has no limit at 0. A student who stops there misses the factor \(x^2\), which forces the product to 0. OpenStax Calculus Volume 1 §2.3.

Key

The bounds go to the same place. The middle function follows them.

The same trap works with a constant factor in front of \(x^2\). The bounds are then a constant times \(\pm x^2\), and both still approach 0.

07Formula sheet

  • A polynomial limit equals the value. Substitute.
  • The two-sided limit exists only when the left-hand and right-hand limits agree.
  • After cancelling a common factor, evaluate what remains.
  • \(\lim(f+g)=\lim f+\lim g\), and \(\lim(cf)=c\lim f\), when the pieces exist.
  • Continuous at \(a\) means \(\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)\).
  • For \(f(x)=mx\) at 0, \(\delta=\varepsilon/|m|\).
  • If \(-x^2\le f(x)\le x^2\) and both bounds approach 0, then \(f\) approaches 0.
