1Model and interpretation

Key relation
\[r=(G-B)/t,\quad\sigma_r=\sqrt{G+B}/t\]

A detector records discrete events. Subtracting an independent background estimate changes the net signal but does not subtract away statistical uncertainty. For independent Poisson counts, variances add.

Quantities and units

QuantityValue or interpretation
G and BIndependent gross and background counts collected in separate equal-duration acquisitions
tDuration of each acquisition in seconds
r and σᵣEstimated net rate and its standard uncertainty in s⁻¹
Poisson assumptionIndependent, constant-rate events. The variance of a count equals its underlying expected count; this lab estimates it from the observed count

Work the model

  1. State the acquisition times. This lab assumes equal times for the gross and separate background records.
  2. Subtract count rates, not raw counts from unequal exposures. For equal times, the net rate is (G − B)/t.
  3. Use G + B in the variance estimate because the two records fluctuate independently.
  4. A negative background-subtracted estimate can occur in a low-signal record. It does not represent a negative number of emitted events. Interpret uncertainty and detection evidence separately.
Worked example · reason through the setup

A hypothetical detector records 145 gross counts and 25 background counts in separate 12 s exposures. Estimated net rate is 10 s⁻¹. Its standard uncertainty is √170 / 12 ≈ 1.087 s⁻¹, under the independent Poisson assumption.

Common mistake

The uncertainty of G − B is not generally √(G − B).

Common mistake

Detector count rate is not automatically source activity. Efficiency, geometry, dead time and branching can matter.

3Limits and further study

What the model leaves out

Independent Poisson records with equal exposure and plug-in variance estimates. No dead-time correction, efficiency calibration or formal detection-limit procedure is included.

Worked reflection · transfer the idea

Imagine four times the exposure at the same underlying gross and background rates. Explain why expected counts quadruple while the relative counting uncertainty is halved.

Write a prediction, identify the quantity held fixed and test the prediction in the lab. Name one additional physical effect that would require a different model.
