1Model and interpretation

Key relation
\[B=\Delta m c^2,\quad B/A\ \text{compares energy per nucleon}\]

The bound nucleus has less rest mass than its separated nucleons. The missing mass corresponds to binding energy. Proton number identifies the element; mass number counts protons plus neutrons.

Quantities and units

Constants are rounded from NIST CODATA 2022. Keep extra digits during calculation and round the final answer.

Work the model

  1. Choose either nuclear masses or consistent atomic masses. Electron masses cancel only when the bookkeeping is set up correctly.
  2. Find the difference between separated constituents and the bound system. Here the mass defect is supplied as an illustrative input.
  3. Convert atomic mass units to energy using the mass-energy equivalent, then divide by A only when asking for energy per nucleon.
  4. A curve of binding energy per nucleon summarizes trends across real measured nuclei; this lab does not predict that curve from A alone.
Worked example · reason through the setup

For an illustrative nucleus with A = 4 and a supplied mass defect of 0.030 u, total binding energy is about 27.945 MeV and energy per nucleon is about 6.986 MeV. The supplied defect is a classroom input, not a claim about the measured helium-4 mass.

Common mistake

Total binding energy and binding energy per nucleon answer different questions.

Common mistake

Do not mix an atomic mass for one side of a reaction with nuclear masses for the other side without accounting for electrons.

Try it in the lab

Predict a direction of change, alter one input and explain the readout. Use a limiting case or a unit conversion to catch a wrong result. Practice problems give different inputs; use the reasoning above rather than memorizing this example.

3Limits and further study

What the model leaves out

The lab converts a supplied mass defect; it is not a nuclear mass model or a prediction of isotope stability.

Worked reflection · transfer the idea

Keep a supplied mass defect fixed while changing A. Explain why the total binding energy is unchanged but energy per nucleon changes, and why arbitrary inputs do not establish a real isotope’s stability.

Write a prediction, identify the quantity held fixed and test the prediction in the lab. Name one additional physical effect that would require a different model.
