Part I

Position

1A circle, not a box

A hole works if it is close enough to where it belongs. A plus-and-minus tolerance on x and y draws a square box round the true position. The box's corners are \(\sqrt2\) times further out than the middles of its edges, so it accepts some holes that are further off than holes it rejects.

Derivation

Let the drill scatter holes with spread \(\sigma\) in x and in y. The box \(\pm t\) needs both coordinates inside, and the circle of radius \(r\) needs the distance inside. That distance follows a Rayleigh law:

\[ P_\text{box} = \left(2\Phi(t/\sigma) - 1\right)^2, \qquad P_\text{circle} = 1 - e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}. \]

The circle through the box's corners has \(r = \sqrt2\,t\), a diameter of \(2\sqrt2\,t\), and \(\pi/2 = 1.57\) times the box's area.

Worked example · a drill with σ = 0.05 mm

Against a \(\pm 0.1\) mm box, \(2\Phi(2) - 1 = 0.954\) per axis, so \(0.954^2 = 91.1\%\) of holes pass. Against the ⌀0.283 mm circle through its corners, \(1 - e^{-4} = 98.2\%\) pass, and no accepted hole is further off than the box already allowed.

Common mistake

Converting \(\pm t\) to a position zone of diameter \(2t\). That circle sits inside the box and is stricter than it. The equivalent zone passes through the corners: \(2\sqrt2\,t\).

Part II

Process

2Cp and Cpk

A tolerance is only worth writing if the process can hold it. Capability compares the band the drawing allows with the spread the process makes.

Derivation \[ C_p = \frac{\text{USL} - \text{LSL}}{6\sigma}, \qquad C_{pk} = \frac{\min(\text{USL} - \mu,\ \mu - \text{LSL})}{3\sigma}. \]

\(C_p\) asks whether the process would fit if centred. \(C_{pk}\) also counts where it sits. The share out of limits is \(\Phi((\text{LSL} - \mu)/\sigma) + \Phi((\mu - \text{USL})/\sigma)\).

Worked example · pins 15 μm high

Pins are made to \(10 \pm 0.03\) mm with \(\sigma = 7.5\) μm, and the mean sits 15 μm high. \(C_p = 60/45 = 1.33\), but \(C_{pk} = 15/22.5 = 0.667\). The upper limit is only \(2\sigma\) away, so 22,750 pins in a million fall out.

Common mistake

Reading \(C_p\) alone. It ignores where the process is centred, so an off-centre process with a good \(C_p\) can still make scrap.

3Watching a process drift

Tools wear, so a process mean drifts. An \(\bar x\) chart plots the mean of each small subgroup against limits set from the process when it was good.

Derivation

A mean of \(n\) parts varies only \(\sigma/\sqrt n\), so the chart's limits are \(\mu_0 \pm 3\sigma/\sqrt n\). A mean drifting \(\delta\) per subgroup crosses the upper one after \(3\sigma/(\sqrt n\,\delta)\) subgroups. Parts start to fall out when the mean passes \(\text{USL} - 3\sigma\).

Worked example · tool wear on a capable process

With \(\sigma = 6\) μm, \(10 \pm 0.03\) mm limits (\(C_p = 1.67\)), subgroups of five and 1 μm of wear per subgroup, the chart flags at subgroup 9. Pins start to fall out only at subgroup 13.

Common mistake

Taking parts inside the limits as proof the process is fine. With \(C_p = 1.33\) the same wear puts pins out at subgroup 8, before the chart flags at 11. Capability is what gives a chart time to work.

What the model leaves out

The gauge is taken as perfect and the spread as steady. In practice a gauge study comes first, and run rules on the chart react to smaller shifts than a single point past the limit.
