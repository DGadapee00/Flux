Part I

Deciding

1Requirements, then weights

Start from the need, not from a part. Write what the part must do as requirements you can check: a number and a limit, such as deflection at most 0.3 mm under the sensor's load. Write what you would like more of as criteria with weights. Then sketch more than one concept before you judge any.

Derivation

A requirement is pass or fail. A concept that misses one is out, whatever else it does well. Score the rest on each criterion from 1 (worst of the field) to 5 (best), and rank by the weighted mean:

\[ S_j = \frac{\sum_k w_k\, s_{jk}}{\sum_k w_k}. \]
Worked example · the run of 1,000

A sensor bracket can be machined aluminium, bent steel sheet or printed nylon. For a run of 1,000 with deflection at most 0.3 mm, the printed part bends 0.6 mm and is out. With weights 1, 5, 2, 1, 1 on mass, cost, deflection, lead time and heat limit, sheet scores 3.4 and machined 2.6.

Common mistake

Scoring the first concept you thought of against nothing in particular. A machined part is often the first sketch; for a run of 1,000 the bent sheet costs a quarter as much and still meets the stiffness requirement.

2How firm is the choice?

Weights are judgements. Before trusting a ranking, ask how far each weight can move before the leader changes.

Derivation

The leader \(L\) stays ahead of a rival \(j\) while \(\sum_k w_k (s_{Lk} - s_{jk}) > 0\). That is linear in each weight, so holding the others, the crossover is

\[ w_k^{*} = -\frac{\sum_{i \ne k} w_i\,(s_{Li} - s_{ji})}{s_{Lk} - s_{jk}}. \]
Worked example · how far the cost weight can fall

For the run of 1,000, sheet leads while the weight on cost stays at 3 or more. Below 3, the machined part's stiffness, lighter weight and shorter lead time carry it.

Key idea

A choice that flips when one weight moves a little is a choice the numbers have not made. Get better data on the close criteria, or build both and test.

What the model leaves out

Scores run straight from the worst concept to the best, so they say nothing about how good the best one is. A matrix ranks the concepts you have; it cannot find one you did not sketch.
