The idea

A nonconformance is a departure from a requirement. A disposition states what happens to this part. Root cause and corrective action address why it happened and how to prevent recurrence.

Walk through it

  1. Record the exact requirement, finding, location and evidence. Identify and hold affected hardware.
  2. Compare use as is, an approved repair and scrap with the authorized review board. Include strength, access, prior repairs and reinspection.
  3. Record approval and repair evidence. Check that the corrective action addresses the process cause rather than only the visible flaw.

Apparatus and readings

Repair is a new controlled process. Its qualified procedure, strength basis, tool access and inspection plan must fit this joint and its history.

Expected evidence: An authorized disposition links the repair to supporting analysis or tests, reinspection and a closed record.

Investigate: An available welder repairs the flaw before approval, or the same defect returns because no process cause was addressed.

The engineer decides

  • The disposition, with the analysis or test that supports it.
  • The repair method. In NASA’s tests a friction plug weld kept nearly all of the weld’s strength, and a fusion weld repair about two thirds.
  • The root cause, and the corrective action before the next weld. Both are separate from fixing this part.
  • How the repair is inspected: by the same methods as the original weld.

What goes wrong

A repair that makes it worse

A fusion repair melts metal the stir weld had kept solid. It left the highest strain and the lowest strength.

How it is found: In test panels repaired the same way, before the method is allowed on hardware.

A third attempt at one spot

Each repair reheats the metal around it.

How it is found: In the repair record. No more than 2 attempts are allowed without the board.

A fix with no cause

The part was repaired and the process was left as it was.

How it is found: When the same flaw returns on the next part.

Worked example · Build a disposition with a strength consequence

A repair is available today, but the repaired condition has less demonstrated strength than the original joint. You need to evaluate the repaired state and the process cause separately.

  1. Contain the affected part and identify the exact unmet requirement. Record the flaw location, dimensions, inspection evidence and previous repair attempts. Do not erase the original finding when the part is repaired.
  2. For a fictional coupon comparison, the original joint reaches 400 MPa and a repair reaches 300 MPa. Retained strength is 300 / 400 = 75%. That is a comparison of these test results, not a qualified repair allowable or a complete structural assessment.
  3. The authorized review needs the relevant repaired properties, local loads, geometry, fracture behavior, test conditions, repair procedure and reinspection. A fast repair can carry a hidden redesign or qualification cost. Root cause and corrective action address the next part; they do not replace disposition of this one.
Worked example · Choosing a repair

Eddy current finds a short unwelded root near the fitting. Three repairs are offered: a friction plug, a second stir pass, or a manual fusion weld.

  • The fusion weld can be done today. The plug needs tooling set up.
  • The flaw is 15 mm long, in a land sized by proof.
  • This place has had one repair attempt already.
  • The tank flies cold.

Choose a repair and defend it with the strength each one keeps, warm and cold. Say who must approve it and how it is inspected. Say what you would refuse to do, and why.

Reasoning to look for

  • The choice uses the measured strengths, warm and cold.
  • Schedule pressure is named and weighed, neither ignored nor obeyed.
  • The count of repair attempts and the board’s role are stated.
  • The repair is inspected by the same methods as the original weld.

This case is a judgement exercise. The evidence and authorized review determine disposition; it is not an answer the practice engine marks.

The paper

Nonconformance report
What was found, where, and against which requirement.
Review board disposition
The decision, its basis and who approved it. Every stir weld repair and every plug repair needs one.
Repair record
The place, the flaw and the procedure, kept permanently.

Ask on the floor

  • What are the options for this flaw, and what does each cost in strength?
  • What changed in the process after the last nonconformance like this one?

What to produce

  • Requirement departure, location, evidence and containment
  • Repair alternatives, retained properties, local assessment and prior attempts
  • Authorized disposition, repair procedure and reinspection evidence
  • Root-cause investigation, corrective action and evidence it worked
Transfer the idea

A second part shows the same flaw after the first part was repaired successfully. Which evidence would test whether the original root-cause explanation was wrong?

Review checklist

  • I separate containment, disposition and corrective action.
  • I account for the repaired material condition and history.
  • I do not turn a coupon ratio into automatic hardware approval.
  • I include the authorized reviewer and reinspection plan.
