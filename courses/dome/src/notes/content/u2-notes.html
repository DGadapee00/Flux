1Fit-up and fixturing

Understand how the joint stays aligned while the tool loads it.

The idea

Fit-up is the alignment before joining. Mismatch is a height difference between edges. A fixture locates and holds the part; an anvil supports a conventional stir weld from behind.

Walk through it

  1. Identify the load path from tool through joint, support and fixture. Compare a conventional tool with the self-reacting arrangement.
  2. Check joint preparation and cleaning against the approved procedure. Measure gap and mismatch along the joint.
  3. Record the setup, measurement locations and readiness checks. Resolve departures before releasing the setup to weld.

Apparatus and readings

The panel edges sit in the fixture. Gauges check edge gap and relative height. The fixture must hold the seam while providing clearance for the tool.

Expected evidence: Gap and mismatch meet the qualified procedure throughout the seam, and the fixture and tool can follow the path.

Investigate: One clamp hides a large gap or the joint opens under load. A single measurement at the start does not describe the whole seam.

The engineer decides

  • Conventional or self-reacting. A conventional tool pushes against an anvil behind the joint. A self-reacting tool pinches the joint between two shoulders and needs no anvil.
  • The gap and mismatch allowed between edges, and how they are measured before the weld.
  • Cleaning: what the procedure asks for at the joint.
  • Tooling or none. NASA welded parts of this dome with no fixture, using the self-reacting tool.

What goes wrong

Mismatch and peaking

The edges sit at different heights, or meet at an angle. Each bends the joint under load, and the two add.

How it is found: With a template after welding. One may be traded against the other only by analysis.

A joint that opens during the weld

The clamps did not hold against the tool’s force.

How it is found: As a change in the force record, and as voids at inspection.

Worked example · Read a setup sheet before joining

The first panel edge fits. Farther along, the gap grows. Fit-up is a measured condition along the joint, not a statement that the first clamp was tight.

  1. A conventional stir tool reacts its load through the joint into backing support. A self-reacting tool closes much of that load between its shoulders. Both still need the qualified arrangement for alignment and access.
  2. Locate the seam from the setup datums. Verify the procedure revision, preparation, cleaning, support and clamp plan. Record gap and mismatch at the specified locations before welding; do not use force to hide an unexplained geometric departure.
  3. In a fictional setup sheet the maximum gap is 0.20 mm. Readings 0.10, 0.14 and 0.24 mm show a local departure. Hold and investigate panel geometry, fixture condition and measurement before release. The machine cannot qualify an unapproved gap after the fact.

The paper

Setup sheet
The fixture, the clamps, and the gap and mismatch measured before welding.
Readiness check
The record that the machine was checked immediately before the production weld.

Ask on the floor

  • Is this weld made against an anvil or with a self-reacting tool, and why?
  • What gap and mismatch were measured before the weld started?

What to produce

  • Procedure revision, joint preparation and setup identity
  • Load path, support type, clamp positions and tool access
  • Gap and mismatch by location, compared with procedure limits
  • Unresolved setup departure, evidence and readiness decision owner
Transfer the idea

A clamp change improves alignment but blocks the ultrasonic probe. Who needs to review that change before the setup is released?

Review checklist

  • I explain where the tool load goes.
  • I compare multiple locations with the procedure.
  • I distinguish gap from mismatch and avoid averaging away a departure.
  • I resolve setup departures before production joining.

2Weld schedule

Connect machine settings to evidence that a joining process works.

The idea

Friction stir welding uses a rotating tool to soften and move metal without melting it. The WPS is the approved welding procedure; the PQR records the tests used to qualify it.

Walk through it

  1. Locate rotation, travel, plunge or force control, tool geometry and joint thickness on the procedure. Explain what each changes.
  2. Follow a coupon from a trial weld through inspection, sectioning and mechanical testing.
  3. Explain why both ends of the qualified process window need evidence. Do not derive a production schedule from the example sizing calculator.

Apparatus and readings

Rotation turns the tool; travel moves it down the seam. The tool, joint, fixture and control method form one qualified setup. The machine settings alone do not define weld quality.

Expected evidence: Inspection and destructive tests support the documented operating window for the defined material, thickness, tool and joint.

Investigate: A smooth weld surface hides a tunnel or weak bond. A good-looking bead is not a substitute for qualification.

The engineer decides

  • Three settings: how fast the tool turns, how fast it travels, and how deep it plunges.
  • Where the window is. Turning faster or travelling slower makes a hotter weld. Too cold leaves voids. Too hot weakens the weld.
  • The pin tool: its shape, its length against the land, and its service life, all written into the procedure.
  • The range allowed on each setting. Test welds at the coldest and the hottest end of the range must both pass.
  • How many test welds. NASA sizes the population so that, with 95% confidence, 90% of welds meet the design strength.

What goes wrong

A tunnel void along the weld

The weld ran too cold: the tool turned too slowly or travelled too fast for the metal to flow.

How it is found: By ultrasound or X-ray, and in cut sections of test welds.

An unwelded root

The pin was too short for the land, or the plunge too shallow.

How it is found: By dye penetrant on the root side. NASA’s finding was that prevention lies in process control.

A weak bond that looks whole

Low heat let the two sides touch without mixing.

How it is found: Poorly. It is very hard to find by inspection, so the procedure has to prevent it.

Worked example · Separate a process trial from qualification

Two trial coupons were made at different rotation and travel settings. One looks better on the surface. You need to decide what can be inferred and what tests come next.

  1. Rotation in revolutions per minute divided by travel in millimetres per minute gives revolutions per millimetre. A fictional trial at 300 rpm and 100 mm/min has 3 rev/mm. At the same rotation and 150 mm/min it has 2 rev/mm.
  2. This ratio is a comparison aid, not a heat-input or quality model. Tool geometry, control mode, material, thickness, contact, fixture and thermal history also matter. A wider shoulder or a different pin can change behavior even when the ratio stays constant.
  3. Trial coupons connect settings to observations, sectioning, inspection and mechanical tests. Qualification establishes a bounded procedure and its essential variables. Do not choose a production schedule from the ratio, a smooth surface or one strong test.
Worked example · Test panels come in low

This week’s test panels average 8% below last month’s ultimate strength. The procedure did not change.

  • A new plate lot arrived this week.
  • The fixture’s backing anvil was reworked.
  • The same pin tool has been used throughout.
  • Every panel passed inspection.

List what could have changed, most likely first. Say which test or record would settle each. Decide whether production welding continues in the meantime.

Reasoning to look for

  • Material, tool, fixture, machine and test method are each considered.
  • Each cause has a check that would confirm it or clear it.
  • The decision on production is stated, with its risk.
  • The spread of the results is looked at, not only the average.

This case is a judgement exercise. The evidence and authorized review determine disposition; it is not an answer the practice engine marks.

The paper

Welding procedure specification (WPS)
The settings, tool, joint and cleaning for one weld. The current copy stays at the welding station.
Procedure qualification record (PQR)
The test welds made with those settings, and the test results that proved them.

Ask on the floor

  • Which settings are essential variables in this procedure, and what range is each allowed?
  • How many test panels qualified it, and how were they tested?

What to produce

  • Trial identities, material, thickness, tool, fixture and control mode
  • Settings compared, observations and tests for each trial
  • Proposed process window and evidence required at its boundaries
  • Changes requiring requalification and the responsible welding specialist
Transfer the idea

Two trials have the same rev/mm but different rotation, travel and tooling. Explain why the first trial’s qualification cannot automatically be transferred to the second.

Review checklist

  • I keep rotation, travel, force or depth and tool geometry distinct.
  • I calculate rev/mm with consistent units.
  • I do not treat rev/mm as a predictor of weld acceptance.
  • I connect the proposed window to inspection and destructive evidence.

3The weld

Read a production record and make a defensible hold decision.

The idea

A process window is the qualified range of settings. An excursion leaves that range. A weld record ties measurements along the seam to the procedure, tool and part.

Walk through it

  1. Match the part, current WPS, tool identity, demonstrated tool life and readiness record.
  2. Follow entry, steady travel and exit. Compare recorded rotation, travel and the specified force or position controls with their qualified ranges.
  3. Locate excursions along the seam. Hold affected hardware for the authorized review process and preserve the original record.

Apparatus and readings

The tool plunges, travels along the joint and withdraws. The operator monitors procedure-defined controls while the machine records their history. Entry and exit need their own planned treatment.

Expected evidence: The trace remains within the qualified ranges and the start, stop and exit treatment match the approved procedure. Inspection is still required.

Investigate: A force or position excursion coincides with joint movement. Returning to the normal range later does not erase the affected section.

The engineer decides

  • Start and stop: where the tool goes in and where it comes out. Both ends differ from the steady weld.
  • How the exit hole is closed. NASA closes the hole a self-reacting weld leaves with a friction pull plug weld.
  • Changing thickness. A retractable pin lets one weld run through a taper, such as 0.320 to 0.650 inch.
  • What stops a weld: which reading out of range ends the run.

What goes wrong

A setting drifts out of its range

Tool wear, a fixture that gives, or a change in the plate.

How it is found: In the weld record. The part is held for review, and the cause is found before the next weld.

The exit hole

The tool leaves a hole where it withdraws.

How it is found: It is planned for: run off the part, retract the pin, or plug the hole.

A worn pin tool

It ran past the life it was shown to have.

How it is found: In the tool log. A tool at its life is marked and taken out of service.

Worked example · Investigate a trace without inventing a cause

The operator sees a short control excursion. The next samples return to normal. A useful handoff says what happened, where it happened and what must be reviewed; it does not guess that the joint is sound or diagnose the cause from one channel.

  1. Match the tool, WPS revision, readiness check, part identity and joint thickness. Follow entry, steady travel and exit as separate parts of the approved sequence.
  2. A fictional procedure permits a monitored force from 8 to 12 kN inclusive. Samples 9.8, 10.1, 12.4 and 10.0 kN include one high sample. The maximum departure is 0.4 kN. Preserve the time and seam-position record as well as the raw readings.
  3. A force spike can prompt review of fixture motion, alignment, control response, thickness and tool condition. It does not uniquely identify a void. Stop or respond according to the approved procedure, hold affected hardware for review and correlate inspection with the excursion location.

The paper

Weld record
The settings the machine held along the seam, certified to be within the procedure.
Tool log
Each pin tool’s use against its demonstrated life.

Ask on the floor

  • Which reading would make you stop this weld?
  • Where does the tool come out, and how is that hole closed?

What to produce

  • Part, seam, procedure revision, tool identity and readiness
  • Channel, units, recorded limits, excursion and seam location
  • Immediate procedure-defined response and affected hardware
  • Evidence requested, review owner and release conditions
Transfer the idea

The rotation and travel stayed inside their ranges while force moved out of range. Explain why two good channels do not cancel the third, and what synchronized records would help.

Review checklist

  • I distinguish an out-of-range reading from a proven defect.
  • I retain original readings and their seam locations.
  • I state containment without hiding the recovered excursion.
  • I request evidence before assigning root cause or releasing the part.

4Inspection

Explain what an inspection signal can tell you and what it can miss.

The idea

Nondestructive evaluation examines a part without cutting it apart. An indication is a signal to interpret; it is not automatically a rejectable defect. Coverage describes what was actually examined.

Walk through it

  1. Map visual, dimensional, surface and volumetric methods to the flaw types they target.
  2. Follow calibration or reference checks into a scan. Relate a signal to its location, depth, coverage and the approved acceptance criteria.
  3. Compare surface and internal findings with the weld record. Ask whether the method can reliably find the flaws assumed by the analysis.

Apparatus and readings

A probe sends sound into the joint and records returning echoes. A scan relates response to position and depth. Reference checks, coupling and coverage are needed before interpreting indications.

Expected evidence: A qualified method, valid reference checks and documented coverage support interpretation against approved criteria. A quiet screen alone is not acceptance.

Investigate: A repeatable indication needs evaluation; loss of coupling or missing coverage can also hide flaws. A clean penetrant result does not clear an internal flaw.

The engineer decides

  • Which methods. A Class A weld needs visual, dimensional, surface and volumetric inspection.
  • Which flaw each is for. Stir weld flaws are mostly flat and thin, so NASA moved to phased array ultrasound for flaws inside and eddy current arrays at the surface.
  • What X-ray is still for: tunnel voids, and gaps that go through the thickness.
  • The crack size each method is counted on to find, 90% of the time with 95% confidence.

What goes wrong

A root flaw that penetrant cannot see

The root is pressed shut, so the dye does not enter.

How it is found: By eddy current or ultrasound, and by controlling pin depth in the first place.

An inspection that was never proved

The method was assumed to find the flaw size the analysis relies on.

How it is found: By qualifying the method before the first production weld, and keeping the proof.

Smeared metal hiding a crack

Machining or grinding closed the surface over it.

How it is found: By etching before penetrant is applied.

Worked example · Read coverage before reading “no indication”

An ultrasonic report says “no reportable indications.” A coverage map reveals a region near the fitting that the probe could not reach. The text and the map must be reviewed together.

  1. A qualified inspection procedure defines the reference checks, scan plan, coverage, reporting and acceptance criteria. An ultrasonic signal needs context: sound path, position, geometry, coupling and reference response.
  2. Suppose a fictional seam is 1,000 mm long and 80 mm has no demonstrated coverage. Covered length is 920 mm, so coverage is 92%. A quiet response over 92% does not make the unexamined 8% acceptable.
  3. Resolve coverage with the responsible inspection and structural teams. This may require a qualified alternate scan or method, changed access, or another authorized disposition. An indication elsewhere still needs interpretation; high amplitude alone is not a universal defect-size measurement.
Worked example · A void along a gore seam

Phased array ultrasound on a gore seam shows one continuous indication 200 mm long, below the surface, to one side of the weld centre.

  • The weld record shows travel speed at the top of its allowed range for that stretch.
  • The pin tool is near the end of its demonstrated life.
  • Penetrant on both faces shows nothing.
  • The seam before it, made the same day, is clean.

Say what the flaw most likely is, and why. Say what else you would look at before deciding. Then propose a disposition, and what must happen before the next weld.

Reasoning to look for

  • The flaw is tied to the evidence, not guessed.
  • More evidence is asked for before the decision, and each item could change it.
  • The disposition names the repair, the analysis or test behind it, and how it is inspected again.
  • Root cause and corrective action are separate from the fix to this part.
  • The review board is involved, as every stir weld repair requires.

This case is a judgement exercise. The evidence and authorized review determine disposition; it is not an answer the practice engine marks.

The paper

Inspection report
The method, the coverage, what was found and where, signed by a certified inspector.
Proof of capability
The record that the method finds the flaw size it is relied on for.

Ask on the floor

  • Which flaw is each inspection here for, and which flaw does none of them find well?
  • What crack size does the analysis assume the inspection finds?

What to produce

  • Flaw types of concern and the methods selected to detect them
  • Procedure, reference checks, personnel qualification and scan conditions
  • Coverage map, indications and correlation with manufacturing records
  • Unexamined regions, limitations and the authorized resolution
Transfer the idea

A scan has full geometric coverage but loses coupling over one section. Explain why “the probe passed over it” and “the volume was effectively examined” are different claims.

Review checklist

  • I check coverage and reference validity before accepting a scan.
  • I distinguish an indication from a disposition.
  • I explain what each method can miss.
  • I involve qualified inspection and structural reviewers for unresolved evidence.
