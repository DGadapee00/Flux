1Proof and leak test

Distinguish strength evidence, leak evidence and permanent deformation.

The idea

Proof tests demonstrate a specified load condition. Leak tests measure containment. Elastic deformation recovers after unloading; permanent growth can indicate yielding.

Walk through it

  1. Read the approved test plan: load level, temperature, instrumentation, limits, inspection and authorization. Explain the difference between proof and leak objectives.
  2. Compare pressure and deformation histories, including measurements before loading and after unloading.
  3. Interpret leaks, residual growth or procedure departures through the review process. Identify what remains unproved about service life and operating conditions.

Apparatus and readings

A qualified test team uses a reviewed rig with calibrated pressure and temperature sensing, deformation measurements and specified protection. This lesson interprets records; it is not a pressure-test operating procedure.

Expected evidence: The required load history is documented, deformation meets the approved limits, the specified leak criterion is met and required inspections are complete.

Investigate: Pressure reached its target but measurable permanent growth remains. No visible leak does not show that the structure stayed elastic.

The engineer decides

  • The proof pressure: 1.05 times design pressure for a propellant tank, and below yield.
  • What the test is for. It screens workmanship and shows strength. To screen flaws, its factor has to come from fracture mechanics.
  • Warm or cold. This weld is 1.6 times stronger at liquid hydrogen temperature, so a room temperature test is harder on it, and the level may be adjusted for the difference.
  • What is inspected after proof. NASA asks for surface and volumetric inspection of the welds after proof, on the first flight article at least.

What goes wrong

A test that cannot fail

The proof level shows nothing the inspections had not.

How it is found: By asking what flaw would have failed at proof, and whether a smaller one can grow to failure in service.

Yielding at proof

The land was sized on ultimate strength alone.

How it is found: As growth that stays after the pressure is released.

A departure from the test procedure

A gauge, a ramp or a hold was not as planned.

How it is found: It is written up as a nonconformance, like a flaw in the part.

Worked example · Interpret unloading and uncertainty

A test reached its specified pressure and had no visible leak. A diameter measured after unloading differs from its pre-test value. Strength, deformation, leakage and procedure compliance are separate evidence streams.

  1. For a fictional before reading of 500.00 mm and after reading of 500.18 mm, residual growth is 0.18 mm. If each reading has a stated bounded uncertainty of ±0.02 mm, a conservative bound on their difference is ±0.04 mm.
  2. The residual interval is then 0.14 to 0.22 mm, excluding zero under those assumptions. First check common measurement references, temperature, calibration and setup; then involve the structural reviewer. Do not declare a unique physical cause from the two numbers alone.
  3. A leak detector produces a method-specific rate or response that must be compared with the approved test criterion and background. A proof result does not establish leak performance at every service temperature, fatigue life, or acceptability of permanent growth. Actual test media, load history and protection are decisions for the qualified test team.
Worked example · Growth after proof

After the proof test the tank is wider at the dome’s ring than before, by more than the measurement can explain.

  • Proof pressure reached its target and held.
  • The lands were sized on ultimate strength.
  • No leak was found.
  • The welds have not yet been inspected after proof.

Explain what happened, using the weld’s yield and ultimate strength. Say what state the tank is in and what you would do next. Say what should change in the design.

Reasoning to look for

  • Yield at proof is checked with numbers, against the weld and not the plate.
  • The state of the tank is judged: what yielding changed, and what must be inspected.
  • The design fix is stated: which check should have sized the land.
  • The departure is written up as a nonconformance.

This case is a judgement exercise. The evidence and authorized review determine disposition; it is not an answer the practice engine marks.

The paper

Proof test report
The pressure, the hold, the temperature, the measured growth and the result.
Leak test report
The method, the rate measured and the limit.
Post-proof inspection report
The welds inspected again after the test.

Ask on the floor

  • What would this proof test have failed that inspection would have passed?
  • Is the proof level adjusted for testing warm, and by how much?

What to produce

  • Approved proof and leak objectives, load conditions and instrumentation
  • Before, loaded and after readings with units, references and uncertainty
  • Leak evidence, residual growth and any procedure departures
  • Required follow-up inspection, structural review and disposition
Transfer the idea

The residual change is smaller than the uncertainty bound. Explain why that does not prove zero change, and what additional evidence would make the decision clearer.

Review checklist

  • I separate proof, leakage and deformation evidence.
  • I calculate the before/after change and state the uncertainty assumption.
  • I check temperature and measurement setup before attributing cause.
  • I leave test execution and hardware release to the qualified team.

References

  • NASA-STD-5001B, Structural Design and Test Factors of Safety for Spaceflight Hardware: The factors on strength, the proof factor for a propellant tank, and what a proof test is for.
  • NASA-STD-5019A, Fracture Control Requirements for Spaceflight Hardware: Proof testing as a flaw screen, and the inspection asked for after proof.
  • Lockheed Martin Michoud for NASA, Mechanical Properties of Friction Stir Welds in Al 2195-T8 (1999): Measured strength of the weld warm and cold, against a fusion weld, and what each repair kept of it.

2Acceptance

Explain the full build and hand over a traceable part with known limits.

The idea

As-built means what was actually made. Traceability connects that hardware to material, processes and records. Acceptance means the defined requirements have documented evidence.

Walk through it

  1. Trace one seam backward through inspection, production record, procedure, tool and material lot.
  2. Check dimensions, test reports, closed nonconformances and interface requirements. Include handling, protection and handoff instructions from the responsible team.
  3. Give a teach-back: requirements, design, material, forming, machining, joining, inspection, repair and testing. Name unresolved risks and the specialists still needed.

Apparatus and readings

Review records against the acceptance checklist and the actual part identity. A completed lesson notebook is preparation for this review, not a hardware release.

Expected evidence: Each requirement has evidence and an accountable approval. Repairs and limitations stay visible to the next team.

Investigate: The part passes tests but a plate lot is missing, a repair has no closure or the next team receives only the nominal CAD model.

The engineer decides

  • Whether each weld is certified as made within its procedure.
  • Whether every nonconformance is closed, with its disposition and its repair inspected.
  • Whether the records trace from the dome back to each plate, tool and procedure.
  • What the next shop needs to know: the sizes as built, and the repairs and their places.

What goes wrong

A break in traceability

A lot number or a tool was not recorded at the station.

How it is found: At the records review, when a weld cannot be tied to its plate or its procedure.

An open nonconformance

The repair was done and the paper was not.

How it is found: At buy-off. The part does not move until it is closed.

Repairs nobody is counting

Each one was closed on its own.

How it is found: In the running audit of repairs against the length welded, which every shop is asked to keep.

Worked example · Integrate the evidence into a handoff

Your dome exists as hardware and as a set of linked records. The receiving team must know its actual geometry, identity, accepted deviations, inspection state and handling constraints.

  1. Make a matrix with one row per requirement and columns for evidence, revision, part identity, result and approval. A test report belongs to the actual part and its tested configuration, not merely to the project folder.
  2. Trace a seam backward from its inspection record to the production record, WPS/PQR, tool and material lot. Trace forward through any repair, reinspection, proof and acceptance. An open record can block release even when the metal looks finished.
  3. The capstone is a build plan that survives questions. Explain your route, material/process choice, geometry, stock plan, machines, setup, qualification, inspection, nonconformance response and verification. Include costs, supplier capability, lead times, handling, unresolved risks and specialist signoffs. A course checklist measures evidence gathered, not a percentage of real-world competence.

The paper

Data package
Certificates, procedures, weld records, inspection and test reports, and nonconformances.
Weld quality audit
Repairs by place, type and length, against the length of weld made.

Ask on the floor

  • Can you trace this weld to its plate lot, its tool and its procedure?
  • How much repair per length of weld is this shop running?

What to produce

  • Requirements-to-evidence matrix and a seam traceability chain
  • Build sequence with stock, suppliers, equipment, fixtures and cost/lead-time assumptions
  • Failures, detection methods, repair policy and verification evidence
  • As-built handoff, handling constraints, unresolved risks and specialist signoffs
Transfer the idea

Capstone challenge: the pressure requirement rises after panels are machined. The new land requirement exceeds the measured minimum land. One weld has an unresolved excursion and an inspection region is unexamined. Write a revised plan. Identify what can continue, what must stop, the evidence to request and which decisions need reopening. Ask a mentor to challenge the revised plan against the requirements and evidence.

  • Hold hardware release and identify the affected part, drawing revision and unresolved records. Keep administrative planning and evidence collection moving where they do not alter or release the hardware.
  • Reopen the requirement and structural assessment. Recompute the pressure-only checks, then assess the complete load, temperature, fracture and life requirements with the responsible specialists. Trace the new requirement into the drawing and verification plan.
  • The machined land cannot be made thicker by removing more metal. Compare an authorized redesign, a qualified alternate route or new panels. Do not assume a repair restores the required section or properties. Revisit material condition, supplier capacity, fixture, procedure qualification, cost and delivery.
  • Resolve the weld excursion and missing inspection coverage independently. Preserve the process trace, investigate the excursion with correlated records, and obtain a qualified inspection plan or authorized disposition for the inaccessible region. A revised wall calculation does not close either record.
  • Update the requirements-to-evidence matrix, as-built configuration and open-item list. Obtain the required reinspection, analysis, test and approvals for the revised state. Explain what remains unknown and hand over the actual hardware history.

Review checklist

  • I can explain the full route without relying on the lesson text.
  • I tie each acceptance claim to evidence for the actual part and revision.
  • I identify missing skills and the specialists needed to build and release hardware.
  • I can explain how a changed requirement propagates through the build plan.

References
