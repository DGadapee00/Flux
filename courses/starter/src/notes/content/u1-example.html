Part I

Rates

1The net rate

Water flows into a tank at \(q_{in}\) and drains at \(q_{out}\). In a short time \(dt\) the volume gains \(q_{in}\,dt\) and loses \(q_{out}\,dt\). So it changes by their difference.

Derivation \[ \frac{dV}{dt} = q_{in} - q_{out}. \]

With both rates constant, the right side is a constant. The volume grows in a straight line.

Worked example · a tank with a drain

An empty 300 L tank fills at 0.5 L/s and drains at 0.1 L/s. The net rate is 0.4 L/s. It fills in \(300/0.4 = 750\) s.

Common mistake

Dividing by the inflow alone gives 600 s. The drain runs the whole time, so the answer is 750 s.

What the model leaves out

A real drain slows as the water gets shallower. Here the rates are constant.

2Accumulation

Integrate the rate from the start. What is in the tank is what was there plus the net rate times the time.

\[ V(t) = V_0 + (q_{in} - q_{out})\,t. \]
Worked example · after 100 seconds

A tank holds 120 L. It fills at 0.5 L/s and drains at 0.2 L/s. After 100 s it holds \(120 + 0.3 \times 100 = 150\) L.

Key idea

Only the difference of the rates matters, never either one alone.
