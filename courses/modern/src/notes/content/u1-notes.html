1Model and interpretation

Key relation
\[\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}},\quad\Delta t=\gamma\Delta\tau,\quad L=L_0/\gamma\]

Observers agree on the laws of physics but can assign different times and lengths to the same experiment. Proper time is measured by a clock present at both events. It is not a label for whichever observer we prefer.

Quantities and units

Constants are rounded from NIST CODATA 2022. Keep extra digits during calculation and round the final answer.

Work the model

  1. Identify the two events and the frame in which they occur at one location. That clock measures proper time.
  2. Write β = v/c and check that its magnitude is less than one. Compute γ before using time dilation or length contraction.
  3. Use time dilation only for the stated pair of events. A laboratory distance divided by a proper time is not the speed in either frame.
  4. For a moving length, the endpoints must be recorded simultaneously in the measuring frame. The rest length belongs to the frame where the object is at rest.
Worked example · reason through the setup

A clock records 1.5 µs between two ticks while moving at 0.8c relative to a laboratory. The lab assigns γ = 1/√(1 − 0.8²) = 5/3, so its interval is 2.5 µs. A 1.2 m rod at rest with the clock is 0.72 m long in the lab when its endpoints are measured simultaneously there.

Common mistake

Do not say that either observer has a defective clock. They compare different frame measurements.

Common mistake

Time dilation alone does not resolve arbitrary multi-stage trips. Events, simultaneity and any frame changes must be tracked.

3Limits and further study

What the model leaves out

The lab compares inertial frames in special relativity. It does not model gravitational time dilation or a full accelerated journey.

Worked reflection · transfer the idea

Change speed from 0.1c to 0.9c. Explain why replacing γ with one works at low speed but fails near light speed. Identify what remains invariant in the light-clock construction.

Write a prediction, identify the quantity held fixed and test the prediction in the lab. Name one additional physical effect that would require a different model.
