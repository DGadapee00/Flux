1Model and interpretation

Key relation
\[\lambda=h/p,\quad p=\sqrt{2m_eK}\]

An electron has momentum and a wavelength associated with its quantum state. Diffraction is evidence for wave behavior, while individual detections remain localized events. A sinusoidal wave is not the path of a small orbiting ball.

Work the model

  1. Choose the particle mass and decide whether the kinetic energy is small compared with its rest energy.
  2. Use K = p²/(2m) only in the nonrelativistic regime, then calculate λ = h/p.
  3. Connect wavelength to an experimental length such as a lattice spacing or slit separation. The wavelength alone does not determine a complete diffraction pattern.
  4. The lab checks the energy a second way from a finite-difference curvature of a plane wave in the Schrödinger kinetic-energy operator.
Worked example · reason through the setup

For a 150 eV electron, the nonrelativistic wavelength is about 0.100 nm. Raising the kinetic energy by a factor of four doubles momentum and halves wavelength. It does not divide wavelength by four.

Common mistake

Do not mix photon energy E = hc/λ with the kinetic energy of a massive nonrelativistic electron.

Common mistake

A shorter wavelength does not by itself specify the probability of landing at one detector position. The state and apparatus also matter.
