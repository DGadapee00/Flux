1Model and interpretation

Key relation
\[\psi_n=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L),\quad E_n=\frac{n^2h^2}{8mL^2}\]

Boundary conditions restrict which wavefunctions can fit a confining region. In an infinite one-dimensional well, ψ vanishes at both ends and allowed energies are discrete. A stationary energy state has a time-independent position density.

Work the model

  1. State where the potential is zero and where it is infinite. Do not apply the interior sinusoid outside the well.
  2. Use a positive integer n. The n = 0 function would be zero everywhere and cannot be normalized.
  3. Count n − 1 interior nodes, then compute energy using the physical width in metres.
  4. Compare the closed-form energy with the expectation of a discrete kinetic-energy operator. A finite grid introduces an error that shrinks as the grid is refined.
Worked example · reason through the setup

For an electron in a 1.0 nm well, E₁ is about 0.376 eV and E₂ is about 1.504 eV. The first excited state has one interior node. Doubling the width reduces every corresponding energy by a factor of four.

Common mistake

The spacing between successive levels is not constant: Eₙ grows as n².

Common mistake

A stationary state is not a particle sitting still at the peaks of its density.

3Limits and further study

What the model leaves out

Perfectly infinite walls and a single nonrelativistic particle are idealizations. Finite wells have penetration outside the nominal boundaries.

Worked reflection · transfer the idea

Explain why the ground-state energy is nonzero and what happens to confinement energy as the well becomes wider.

Write a prediction, identify the quantity held fixed and test the prediction in the lab. Name one additional physical effect that would require a different model.
