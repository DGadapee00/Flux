1Model and interpretation

Key relation
\[P_+=\cos^2[(\theta-\alpha)/2],\quad P_- =1-P_+\]

A spin-half state gives two possible results for one spin-component measurement. State preparation and analyzer orientation determine probabilities. The Bloch-vector representation organizes those probabilities; it is not the path of a tiny rotating object.

Quantities and units

QuantityValue or interpretation
θ and αPrepared-state and analyzer polar angles in one common plane
Angle conversionRadians = degrees × π/180. Divide the relative angle by two inside the cosine
Plus and minus outcomes+ħ/2 and −ħ/2; the displayed mean is divided by ħ/2

Work the model

  1. Specify the prepared state and the axis being measured. These are different choices.
  2. In the common plane shown, use the half-angle overlap formula for P₊. The angle difference belongs between the prepared state and analyzer axes.
  3. Form the expectation from both possible outcomes: (ħ/2)P₊ − (ħ/2)P₋. An expectation need not itself be one of the observed outcomes.
  4. Check the same probability from the Bloch-vector dot product. Do not replace a sequence of measurements with a single classical projection.
Worked example · reason through the setup

Prepare spin up along one axis and set the analyzer 90° away. The two probabilities are both 1/2 and the mean component is zero. Individual results are still +ħ/2 or −ħ/2, not zero.

Common mistake

A zero expectation value does not imply that each measurement returns zero.

Common mistake

Spin is intrinsic angular momentum, not literal surface rotation of a small solid sphere.

3Limits and further study

What the model leaves out

Pure spin-half states and ideal projective measurements in one plane. The lab does not model mixed states, azimuthal phase, entanglement or detector imperfections.

Worked reflection · transfer the idea

Compare aligned, perpendicular and opposite analyzers. Explain which are deterministic and which split the outcomes equally.

Write a prediction, identify the quantity held fixed and test the prediction in the lab. Name one additional physical effect that would require a different model.
