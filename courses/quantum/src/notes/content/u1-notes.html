1Model and interpretation

Key relation
\[\int|\psi|^2dx=1,\quad\Delta x\Delta p\geq\hbar/2\]

The wavefunction contains amplitudes. Its squared magnitude is a probability density, which must be integrated over a region to obtain a probability. For this Gaussian family, position and momentum uncertainties attain the minimum product.

Work the model

  1. Separate amplitude from probability density and keep their units distinct. A density at one point is not a finite probability.
  2. Normalize the density over the whole line. The lab integrates a finite interval extending eight standard deviations in each direction.
  3. Calculate Δk = 1/(2σ) and Δp = ħΔk, converting inverse nanometres to inverse metres.
  4. Changing the central wave number adds phase variation and shifts mean momentum while leaving this position density unchanged.
Worked example · reason through the setup

Choose σ = 0.4 nm. Then Δk = 1.25 nm⁻¹ and Δp is about 1.318 × 10⁻²⁵ kg·m/s. Multiplying by 0.4 × 10⁻⁹ m gives ħ/2. Changing the carrier wave number does not alter that uncertainty product.

Common mistake

Do not read the real part of ψ as a probability density. It can be negative.

Common mistake

Uncertainty describes the distribution of outcomes in a state. It is not merely an instrument specification.

3Limits and further study

What the model leaves out

A one-dimensional normalized Gaussian packet is shown at one instant. Time evolution, dispersion and non-Gaussian states are further work.

Worked reflection · transfer the idea

Compare a narrow packet with a broad packet at the same central wave number. Explain what changes in momentum uncertainty and what does not change in mean momentum.
