## Units

**Unit 1. Static failure**

- Stress review; ductile criteria (maximum shear, distortion energy); brittle criteria (Coulomb–Mohr);
  factor of safety; stress concentration in static loading.
- `failure-envelope`: the σ₁–σ₂ plane with Tresca's hexagon and von Mises' ellipse and a load point
  the student drags. Shows where the two disagree most (pure shear) and that both agree on
  uniaxial tension. Check: the safety factor from the envelope against the formula.
- Traps: Tresca applied with the in-plane maximum shear when the third principal stress is zero and
  the other two share a sign.

**Unit 2. Fatigue**

- S–N curves; the endurance limit and Marin factors; notch sensitivity; mean stress (Goodman,
  Soderberg, Gerber); cumulative damage (Miner).
- `goodman`: alternating against mean stress with the three lines. Shows that a tensile mean stress
  lowers the alternating stress a part can take. Check: the safety factor from the line's intercept
  geometry against the formula.
- `notch`: a filleted shaft. Shows K_t rising as the fillet radius shrinks, and K_f below K_t for a
  notch-insensitive material. Data: a stated curve fit, cited.

**Unit 3. Shafts**

- Combined bending and torsion; fatigue design of shafts; critical speed.
- `shaft`: a shaft with two gears or pulleys. Shows that once K_f is included, the critical section
  can be a shoulder rather than the point of largest moment. Check: the moment diagram from `mechanics/sections`.

**Unit 4. Bolted joints**

- Thread geometry; preload; bolt and member stiffness; the joint diagram; separation; fatigue of
  bolts.
- `bolted-joint`: the joint diagram with preload and an external load. Shows that the bolt carries
  only a fraction C of the external load until the joint separates. Check: the stiffness ratio from
  both the frustum model and a simpler cylinder model, stated as an estimate.
- Application lab, `flange`: a bolted pipe flange under internal pressure. The pressure on the
  sealed area gives the separating load; the bolt count and size give the preload needed to keep
  the joint closed with a stated safety factor. Standard flange dimensions stated with a source.

**Unit 5. Springs**

- Helical compression springs; spring index; the Wahl factor; deflection; buckling; fatigue.
- `spring`: a helical spring with wire and coil diameter. Shows stiffness falling with D³ and shear
  stress rising with the index. Check: k from the energy method against the formula.
- Application: an engine valve spring, sized for its lift and checked for surge and fatigue.

**Unit 6. Bearings and gears**

- Rolling-contact bearing life (L₁₀, the cube law); journal bearings in brief; spur-gear geometry;
  Lewis bending stress.
- `bearing-life`: life against load. Shows that doubling the load cuts ball-bearing life by 8.
  Check: the cube law against the catalogue equation.
- `gear-tooth`: Lewis bending stress against module and face width. Check: the beam-in-bending
  estimate at the tooth root.
