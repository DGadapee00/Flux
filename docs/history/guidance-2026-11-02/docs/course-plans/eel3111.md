## Units

**Unit 1. Basic laws**

- Charge, current, voltage, power and the passive sign convention; Ohm's law; KCL and KVL; series
  and parallel; voltage and current dividers; Δ–Y.
- `kirchhoff`: a circuit with draggable sources and resistors, node voltages and branch currents
  drawn. Shows that the sum of powers is zero, so some element must deliver power. Recompute: MNA.
  Check: KCL at every node and KVL around every mesh, as residuals.
- Problem families: equivalent resistance where the topology varies (bridge, ladder, Δ–Y needed);
  divider problems where the unknown varies; power sign questions (absorbed or delivered).
- Traps: the passive sign convention backwards; treating two resistors as in series when a node
  between them branches.

**Unit 2. Methods of analysis**

- Nodal analysis (with supernodes); mesh analysis (with supermeshes); dependent sources.
- `nodal-mesh`: the same circuit solved both ways side by side. Shows that the two methods give the
  same branch currents with different equation counts. Check: the two solutions against each other.
- Problem families: set up and solve with a dependent source; choose the method with fewer
  equations; supernode and supermesh configurations.
- Traps: a dependent source's controlling variable written in the wrong direction.

**Unit 3. Circuit theorems**

- Linearity and superposition; source transformation; Thévenin and Norton; maximum power transfer.
- `thevenin`: a load swept across a network. Shows that the terminal V–I line is all the network is
  to the load, and that its intercepts are V_th and I_N. Check: open-circuit voltage and short-circuit
  current from MNA against the swept line.
- `max-power`: P_load against R_load. Shows the peak at R_L = R_th, at 50% efficiency.
- Application lab, `bridge`: a strain-gauge load cell on a Wheatstone bridge, as in a digital scale.
  Shows that the bridge output is millivolts and linear in strain only for small ΔR/R. Real gauge
  factor and excitation stated. What it leaves out: temperature drift and the amplifier.
- Problem families: Thévenin with and without dependent sources (test-source method); superposition
  with two sources where each answer part removes a different source.
- Traps: replacing a current source with a short when turning it off; including dependent sources
  in the "turn off" step.

**Unit 4. Operational amplifiers**

- The ideal op amp; inverting, non-inverting, summing and difference amplifiers; cascades;
  saturation.
- `opamp`: configurations with gain and supply sliders. Shows that the ideal model holds until the
  output hits the rails. Check: a finite-gain model's output approaching the ideal as A → ∞.
- Application: the instrumentation amplifier after the load-cell bridge (Unit 3's lab gains this
  stage).

**Unit 5. Capacitors, inductors and first-order circuits**

- Energy storage; series and parallel C and L; source-free and step responses of RC and RL
  circuits; the time constant; switching.
- `rc-rl`: a switched RC or RL circuit. Shows that the capacitor voltage and the inductor current
  are continuous through a switch, and that 5τ is "settled". Recompute: the closed form. Check: RK4
  on the circuit's ODE.
- Application lab, `solenoid`: a solenoid valve driven from a supply. Shows that the current, and so
  the pull, rises with τ = L/R, delaying the valve, and why a flyback diode is needed when it
  switches off. A real coil's L and R stated with a source.
- Problem families: initial and final values after a switch; τ from a circuit with several
  resistors (Thévenin seen by C or L); time to reach a value.
- Traps: using the source resistance alone for τ instead of the Thévenin resistance the element
  sees; assuming the capacitor current is continuous.

**Unit 6. Second-order circuits**

- Series and parallel RLC; natural and step responses; overdamped, critically damped and
  underdamped.
- `rlc`: series or parallel RLC with R as the slider. Shows the three regimes and the critical R.
  Check: RK4, and the analogy with MAP 2302's `oscillator`.

**Unit 7. Sinusoidal steady state** (confirm against the syllabus; some sections move this to
Circuit Analysis II)

- Phasors; impedance and admittance; phasor nodal and mesh analysis; AC power (average, reactive,
  power factor).
- `phasor`: rotating phasors and the time waveforms they project. Shows that KVL holds for phasors,
  not for magnitudes. Check: RK4 steady state against the phasor solution.
