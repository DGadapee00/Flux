## Units

**Unit 1. Direct stiffness**

- Springs and bars; element and global stiffness matrices; assembly; boundary conditions; reactions.
- `assembly`: a spring or truss chain whose global K fills in as elements are added. Shows that K is
  singular until the structure is supported. Check: the truss's member forces from `mechanics/truss`.

**Unit 2. The weak form and 1D elements**

- Strong and weak forms; Galerkin's method; linear and quadratic shape functions; convergence.
- `bar-refine`: a bar under a distributed load meshed with n linear elements. Shows that nodal
  displacements are exact while stresses are piecewise constant and converge at the element's rate.
  Check: the exact quadratic solution and the error's slope on log–log axes.

**Unit 3. Beams and frames**

- Hermite beam elements; frames; equivalent nodal loads.
- `beam-element`: a beam meshed with one to many elements. Shows that a point-loaded beam is exact
  with one element per span, and a distributed load needs equivalent nodal loads. Check: EGN 3331's
  `elastic` solutions.

**Unit 4. Two-dimensional elements**

- Plane stress and plane strain; CST and Q4; isoparametric mapping; Gauss quadrature; shear locking.
- `locking`: a cantilever in bending meshed with CST, Q4 and Q8. Shows CST and full-integration Q4
  far too stiff on a coarse mesh. Check: beam theory's tip deflection.
- `plate-hole`: a plate with a hole in tension, refined toward the hole. Shows the peak stress
  converging to K_t = 3 only with a fine mesh there. Check: Kirsch's solution.

**Unit 5. Heat conduction by finite elements**

- The conduction weak form; convection boundaries; transient conduction in brief.
- `fe-heat`: a fin solved by elements. Check: EML 4140's `fin` closed form.

**Unit 6. Dynamics and modelling judgment**

- Mass matrices; natural frequencies; mesh convergence studies; singularities; symmetry; checking a
  result by hand.
- `modes`: a cantilever's first three modes from the element model. Check: the Euler–Bernoulli
  frequencies.
- Application lab, `thick-wall`: the engine's chamber wall under pressure, modelled axisymmetrically.
  Shows where the thin-wall formula σ = pr/t is close and where Lamé's thick-wall solution is
  needed. Check: Lamé's closed form.
