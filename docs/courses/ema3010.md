## Units

**Unit 1. Bonding and crystal structure**

- Bonding types; FCC, BCC, HCP; coordination number and packing factor; density; Miller indices;
  X-ray diffraction.
- `unit-cell`: FCC, BCC and HCP cells in 3D with atoms that can be shrunk. Shows the atoms touching
  along the face diagonal (FCC) or the body diagonal (BCC), which fixes a in terms of R. Check: the
  packing factor computed from the geometry and by Monte Carlo filling.
- `bragg`: a beam on lattice planes. Shows peaks only where nλ = 2d sin θ. Check: d from the Miller
  indices and a.

**Unit 2. Defects and diffusion**

- Vacancies (Arrhenius); dislocations; Fick's first and second laws; the erf solution; temperature
  dependence of D.
- `diffusion`: carburising a steel surface. Shows that the depth for a given concentration grows
  with √(Dt), so doubling the depth takes four times as long. Check: a finite-difference solution of
  Fick's second law.

**Unit 3. Mechanical properties and strengthening**

- Stress–strain (links EGN 3331's `tension-test`); hardness; dislocations and slip; strengthening by
  grain size, solid solution, strain hardening.
- `hall-petch`: yield strength against grain size. Shows strength rising as d^{−1/2}. Data: stated
  constants, cited.

**Unit 4. Failure**

- Fracture toughness and the critical crack; ductile-to-brittle transition; fatigue; creep.
- `crack`: a plate with a crack. Shows the critical crack length falling with the square of the
  stress. Check: K = Yσ√(πa) solved for a.

**Unit 5. Phase diagrams and heat treatment**

- Binary isomorphous and eutectic diagrams; the lever rule; the iron–carbon diagram;
  microstructures; TTT diagrams in brief.
- `lever-rule`: a binary diagram with a draggable state point. Shows phase fractions from the tie
  line, and that the fractions are not the compositions. Check: mass balance of each component.

**Unit 6. Material classes and selection**

- Ceramics, polymers and composites; rule of mixtures; selection charts and performance indices.
- `composite`: fibre fraction against modulus, iso-strain and iso-stress. Shows the bounds and where
  real composites fall. Check: both bounds from the rule of mixtures.
- Application lab, `chamber-material`: copper, stainless steel and a nickel alloy for the engine's
  chamber, compared on conductivity, strength at temperature and melting point. Shows why a
  heat-sink chamber favours conductivity and a thin-walled one favours hot strength. Property values
  stated with sources. Links EML 4140's `heat-sink`.
