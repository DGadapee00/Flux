## Units

**Unit 1. First-order equations** (18.03SC Unit I)

- Separable equations; direction fields and isoclines; Euler's method; linear equations and the
  integrating factor; autonomous equations, the phase line and stability; existence and uniqueness.
- `slope-field`: a direction field with a draggable initial point. Shows that solutions never cross
  where the theorem holds, and what happens where it fails (y′ = √|y|). Recompute: RK4 from the
  point. Check: the closed form where one exists.
- `euler`: Euler's method against the exact solution. Shows that halving h halves the error (first
  order), and that a big step on y′ = −ky oscillates and blows up past h = 2/k. Check: the error
  ratio on a log–log plot has slope 1.
- `phase-line`: logistic and harvesting models. Shows that equilibria and their stability come from
  the sign of f(y), with no solving. Check: RK4 trajectories land on the stable equilibria.
- Application lab, `blowdown`: a GOX bottle emptying through a choked orifice. Choked mass flow is
  proportional to pressure, so isothermal blowdown is a first-order linear decay with a time
  constant set by bottle volume, orifice C_d·A and temperature. Shows why the stand's run time falls
  off as the bottle empties. What it leaves out: adiabatic cooling (the gas cools, which the stand
  sim models), and the regulator. Check: against the stand sim's blowdown at matching inputs.
- Problem families: classify (separable, linear, autonomous, none); solve an IVP; read stability
  from a phase line; mixing-tank and Newton-cooling models where the unknown varies (concentration,
  time to reach a value, inflow rate).
- Traps: forgetting the constant before applying the initial condition; multiplying only one side
  by the integrating factor; losing the equilibrium solution when dividing by y.

**Unit 2. Second-order linear equations** (18.03SC Unit II)

- Constant coefficients; the characteristic equation; real, repeated and complex roots; damping;
  undetermined coefficients; the exponential response formula; resonance; frequency response, gain
  and phase lag; LRC circuits.
- `oscillator`: mass–spring–damper with ζ and ω_n sliders. Shows the three regimes and that critical
  damping returns fastest without overshoot. Recompute: the closed form for each root case. Check:
  RK4 of the same equation.
- `resonance`: sinusoidal forcing with a frequency sweep. Shows that the peak gain sits below ω_n
  when damped, and that the phase passes −90° at ω_n. Check: steady-state amplitude from a long
  simulation against the gain formula.
- Application lab, `regulator-spring`: a pressure regulator's poppet as a mass on a spring with gas
  damping. Shows when it rings (chatter) and what damping stops it. Real spring rate and poppet mass
  from the engine repo's regulator if listed; otherwise stated as typical.
- Problem families: solve for each root case; find the form of a particular solution (what to
  guess, including the t·e^{rt} case); find ω for peak gain; match a graph to its equation.
- Traps: guessing A cos ωt alone when the equation has a damping term; missing the t factor at
  resonance; writing e^{(a±bi)t} solutions without taking real parts.

**Unit 3. Fourier series and the Laplace transform** (18.03SC Unit III)

- Fourier series of periodic inputs; step and delta functions; impulse response; convolution;
  the Laplace transform; partial fractions; solving IVPs; transfer functions; poles and amplitude
  response.
- `fourier`: a square or sawtooth wave built from N terms. Shows the Gibbs overshoot staying near 9%
  as N grows. Check: the overshoot against its limit.
- `laplace`: an IVP solved by transform, with its poles plotted. Shows that pole positions set the
  response's decay and frequency. Check: the inverse transform against RK4.
- `convolution`: input, impulse response and output on one screen. Shows the output as a weighted
  memory of past input. Check: convolution by quadrature against the Laplace solution.
- Problem families: transform and invert (with a shift or a step); solve an IVP with a step input;
  find a transfer function and its poles; match pole maps to responses.
- Traps: the sign of the initial-condition terms in L{y″}; partial fractions with a repeated root;
  the time shift without the step function.

**Unit 4. First-order systems** (18.03SC Unit IV)

- Linear systems; eigenvalues and normal modes; phase portraits (node, saddle, spiral, centre);
  matrix exponential; nonlinear systems; linearization at critical points; limit cycles.
- `phase-portrait`: a 2×2 linear system with editable entries. Shows that the trace and determinant
  alone decide the portrait type. Recompute: eigen-decomposition. Check: RK4 trajectories.
- `linearize`: the damped pendulum, a predator–prey model, and ẋ = −y + ax(x² + y²),
  ẏ = x + ay(x² + y²). Shows that a node, saddle or spiral in the linearization carries over, and
  that a linear centre decides nothing: the last system's linearization is a centre for every a,
  but it spirals out for a > 0 and in for a < 0. Check: RK4 of the full system.
- Problem families: classify from (trace, determinant); solve a system with real or complex
  eigenvalues; find and classify critical points of a nonlinear system.
- Traps: eigenvector of the wrong eigenvalue; calling a linear centre stable for the nonlinear
  system.
