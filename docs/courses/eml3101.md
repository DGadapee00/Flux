## Units

**Unit 1. Exergy** (if not finished in EML 3100)

- Reversible work, irreversibility, exergy of a closed system and a flow; second-law efficiency.
- `exergy`: heat delivered at temperature T. Shows that the same kilojoule is worth less the closer T
  is to the surroundings. Check: the entropy balance's T₀·S_gen against the exergy destroyed.

**Unit 2. Gas power cycles**

- Air-standard assumptions; Otto; Diesel; Brayton, with regeneration, intercooling and reheat;
  the jet-propulsion cycle.
- `otto-diesel`: both cycles on P–v and T–s. Shows that Otto's efficiency depends only on the
  compression ratio and k, and that Diesel's is lower at the same ratio. Recompute: cold-air
  standard formulas. Check: the cycle integral ∮δQ = ∮δW and the variable-c_p route through NASA-7.
- `brayton`: pressure ratio and turbine inlet temperature. Shows that net work peaks at one
  pressure ratio while efficiency keeps rising. Check: the optimum r_p = (T₃/T₁)^{k/(2(k−1))}
  against a numerical maximum.
- Traps: the compression ratio of volumes used as a pressure ratio; k for air applied at
  combustion temperatures without comment.

**Unit 3. Vapour power cycles**

- Ideal and actual Rankine; reheat; regeneration with open and closed feedwater heaters.
- `rankine`: a Rankine cycle on T–s with reheat and a feedwater heater. Shows why reheat raises
  turbine-exit quality and regeneration raises efficiency. Recompute: IF97 states. Check: the energy
  balance of every component and of the cycle.
- Traps: pump work neglected when it is asked for; quality above 1 not caught.

**Unit 4. Refrigeration and heat pumps** (needs R-134a, D10)

- Ideal and actual vapour compression; COP; heat pumps.
- `vapour-compression`: the cycle on P–h and T–s. Shows that COP falls as the temperature lift grows.
  Check: COP against the Carnot COP for the same temperatures.

**Unit 5. Gas mixtures and psychrometrics**

- Mass and mole fractions; Dalton and Amagat; properties of ideal-gas mixtures; humidity;
  dew point; the psychrometric chart; air-conditioning processes.
- `psychrometric`: the chart generated from the equations, with processes drawn. Shows that cooling
  below the dew point removes water. Check: each process's mass and energy balance.

**Unit 6. Chemical reactions and combustion**

- Fuels; air–fuel ratio; theoretical and excess air; enthalpy of formation; first-law analysis of
  reacting systems; adiabatic flame temperature.
- `flame`: CH₄ with O₂ or air across the mixture ratio. Shows that the flame temperature peaks
  near stoichiometric and that diluting with nitrogen lowers it sharply. Recompute: an energy
  balance on NASA-7 enthalpies, no dissociation. Check: the energy balance closes; the stoichiometric
  ratio from atom balance.
- Application lab, `methalox`: the 25 lbf engine's O/F of 2.8 against the no-dissociation flame
  temperature and the engine repo's CEA chamber temperature. Shows that real combustion at these
  temperatures dissociates, so the simple balance overshoots. The CEA number is the design value;
  the course model is the lesson. Uses the engine repo's reactant enthalpy (−74.873 kJ/mol for CH₄;
  DECISIONS.md, D-A1).
- Traps: mass-based and mole-based ratios mixed; water's enthalpy of formation taken as liquid when
  the products are gas.

**Unit 7. Compressible flow** (if UNF covers it here)

- Stagnation properties; speed of sound; isentropic flow in nozzles; choking; normal shocks.
- `nozzle`: a converging–diverging nozzle with back-pressure. Shows the choked throat and the shock
  moving into the nozzle as back-pressure rises. Uses the `thermo` isentropic functions already
  checked against NACA 1135.
- Application: the engine's nozzle at ε = 3, at sea level and at altitude.
