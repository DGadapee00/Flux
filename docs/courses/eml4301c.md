## Units

**Unit 1. Feedback and steady-state error**

- Open and closed loop; block-diagram algebra; sensitivity; system type and steady-state error;
  disturbance rejection.
- `loop`: a DC motor speed loop with a gain slider and a load disturbance. Shows that more gain
  shrinks the error but never removes it for a type-0 plant. Check: the final-value theorem against
  the simulation.
- Application lab, `cruise`: a car's cruise control on a hill. Shows that P control lets the speed
  sag on the climb (steady-state error) and PI brings it back. Real car mass, drag and grade
  stated with a source.

**Unit 2. PID control**

- P, PI, PD and PID; tuning; integrator windup.
- `pid`: PID on a motor position loop. Shows that I removes the steady-state error and adds
  overshoot, and D adds damping. A saturation switch shows windup. Check: the closed-loop poles
  against the simulated response.

**Unit 3. Stability and root locus**

- Routh–Hurwitz; root-locus rules; design by root locus.
- `root-locus`: the locus with a gain slider moving the closed-loop poles, step response beside it.
  Shows where the loop goes unstable. Check: Routh's critical gain against the locus crossing.

**Unit 4. Frequency-domain design**

- Bode and Nyquist; gain and phase margins; lead and lag compensation.
- `margins`: a loop's Bode plot with the margins marked and a delay slider. Shows that a delay eats
  phase margin until the loop rings, then oscillates. Check: margins against the closed-loop step's
  overshoot (approximate relation, stated as such).

**Unit 5. Actuators, sensors and digital control**

- DC motor model; encoders; sampling; discretised controllers; the effect of sample rate.
- `sampling`: the PID of Unit 2 run at a chosen sample rate. Shows the loop degrading as sampling
  slows. Check: the discrete simulation against the continuous one at fast sampling.
