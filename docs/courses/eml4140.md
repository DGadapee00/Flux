## Units

Readings are Lienhard sections, as 2.51 assigns them.

**Unit 1. Modes and thermal resistance** (Lienhard Ch 1–2)

- Conduction, convection, radiation; Fourier's law; the resistance analogy; plane walls, cylinders
  and spheres; contact resistance; the critical radius of insulation.
- `wall`: a composite wall as a resistance network. Shows that the largest resistance sets the heat
  flow and takes the largest temperature drop. Check: the 1D conduction equation solved by finite
  differences.
- `insulation`: insulation on a thin pipe. Shows that adding insulation can increase heat loss below
  the critical radius k/h. Check: the derivative of total resistance.
- Traps: the area of a cylindrical wall taken as flat; adding conductances in series.

**Unit 2. Steady conduction and fins** (Lienhard Ch 4, §4.5)

- The heat equation; boundary conditions; 2D conduction by shape factors and numerics; fins and fin
  efficiency.
- `fin`: a pin fin with length and material sliders. Shows that length past mL ≈ 2 adds almost nothing
  (tanh 2 = 0.96), and that a low-k fin can be worse than none. Recompute: the closed form with a
  convective tip. Check: finite differences.

**Unit 3. Transient conduction** (Lienhard §1.3, Ch 5)

- Lumped capacity and the Biot number; series solutions for slabs, cylinders and spheres;
  semi-infinite solids; multidimensional products.
- `lumped`: a body cooling in a fluid. Shows that the lumped model holds while Bi < 0.1. Recompute:
  the exponential. Check: the first-term series solution and finite differences.
- `semi-infinite`: two bodies touching. Shows the contact temperature set by √(kρc), which is why
  metal feels colder than wood at the same temperature. Check: the erf solution by quadrature.
- Application lab, `heat-sink`: the 25 lbf engine's chamber wall as a heat sink. Shows how long the
  wall can run before it reaches its limit, and why the throat runs out first. Recompute: lumped
  transient with the heat flux from Unit 4's Bartz estimate. What it leaves out: film cooling, axial
  conduction. Check: a 1D transient through the wall by finite differences.

**Unit 4. Forced convection** (Lienhard Ch 6–7)

- Boundary layers and the heat-transfer coefficient; laminar and turbulent plates; internal flow
  (developing and fully developed, constant flux and constant wall temperature); crossflow.
- `plate`: thermal and velocity boundary layers. Shows that Pr sets their relative thickness.
  Check: the integral method against the similarity solution.
- `tube`: internal flow with constant wall temperature. Shows the log-mean temperature difference and
  the bulk temperature's exponential approach. Check: marching the energy balance down the tube.
- Application: the Bartz estimate of throat heat flux, derived from the turbulent-pipe correlation,
  with real chamber pressure, throat diameter and c* from the engine's design table. Check: the
  engine repo's own estimate if it has one.

**Unit 5. Natural convection** (Lienhard §§8.1–8.4)

- Buoyancy-driven flow; Grashof and Rayleigh; vertical plates; enclosures.
- `natural`: a heated vertical plate. Shows h growing with ΔT^{1/4} in laminar flow.

**Unit 6. Heat exchangers** (Lienhard Ch 3)

- Overall coefficient; LMTD; ε–NTU; parallel, counter and crossflow.
- `exchanger`: counterflow against parallel flow. Shows that counterflow can heat the cold stream
  above the hot outlet temperature. Check: LMTD and ε–NTU give the same duty.

**Unit 7. Radiation** (Lienhard Ch 10)

- Black bodies; emissivity; view factors; gray-body networks; shields.
- `view-factor`: two surfaces with movable geometry. Shows reciprocity and the summation rule.
  Check: Monte Carlo ray counting against the closed form.
- `shield`: radiation shields between two plates. Shows each shield cutting the exchange.
  Check: the network solution against direct enclosure analysis.
