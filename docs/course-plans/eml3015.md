## Units

**Unit 1. Properties and hydrostatics**

- Density, viscosity, surface tension; pressure at a point; manometry; forces on plane and curved
  submerged surfaces; buoyancy and stability.
- `gate`: a submerged plane gate. Shows that the resultant acts below the centroid, and moves toward
  it as depth grows. Recompute: F = p_c A and y_cp = y_c + I/(y_c A). Check: the pressure
  distribution integrated by quadrature.
- `stability`: a floating body tilted. Shows that stability depends on the metacentre above G, not
  on the centre of buoyancy above G. Check: the righting moment computed from the shifted buoyancy.
- EML 3100's `manometer` is linked, not rebuilt.
- Traps: the centroid's depth used for the centre of pressure; gauge and absolute pressure mixed.

**Unit 2. Bernoulli and the energy equation**

- Streamlines; Bernoulli and its assumptions; stagnation, static and dynamic pressure; flow
  meters (Pitot, venturi, orifice); the energy equation with head loss, pumps and turbines.
- `venturi`: flow through a converging–diverging duct. Shows that pressure falls where speed rises,
  and recovers only partly when there is loss. Check: mass conservation and Bernoulli solved
  separately agree at every station.
- Application lab, `orifice-meter`: an orifice plate metering water in a pipe. Shows how the
  discharge coefficient C_d comes from measured flow and Δp, and why the meter loses pressure a
  venturi does not. Standard plate geometry stated with a source.
- Traps: Bernoulli across a pump or through a loss; velocity head at a tank surface not set to zero.

**Unit 3. Control volumes: mass, momentum and energy**

- Reynolds transport theorem; mass, linear momentum and angular momentum for control volumes.
- `jet-vane`: a jet on a fixed or moving vane, and a pipe reducer held by a flange. Shows that the
  force comes from the change in momentum flux and the pressure on the control surface. Check: the
  same force from a control volume drawn differently.
- Application lab, `hose`: a fire hose and nozzle as a control volume. Shows the force the crew must
  hold, from the change in momentum flux and the pressure at the coupling. Real flow and nozzle size
  stated with a source. Check: the same force from a control volume drawn differently.
- Traps: leaving out the pressure term; using the absolute velocity for a moving vane.

**Unit 4. Dimensional analysis and similitude**

- Buckingham Π; common groups (Re, Fr, Ma, Eu); model testing.
- `similitude`: a model and prototype with sliders. Shows that matching Re and Fr at once is usually
  impossible with the same fluid. Check: the Π groups computed both ways.

**Unit 5. Internal flow**

- Laminar and turbulent pipe flow; entrance length; the Moody chart and Colebrook; minor losses;
  pipe systems in series and parallel; pump selection.
- `pipe`: velocity profile and pressure drop against Re. Shows the parabolic profile, f = 64/Re, and
  the turbulent plateau set by roughness. Recompute: Colebrook by iteration. Check: Haaland's
  explicit form within its stated error; Hagen–Poiseuille by quadrature of the profile.
- `network`: two pipes in parallel. Shows that flow splits so the head losses are equal, not the
  flows. Check: Hardy Cross iteration against a Newton solve.
- Application: a house's water supply, from the meter to an upstairs shower, with fittings. Shows
  why the shower loses pressure when another tap opens.
- Traps: the Darcy and Fanning factors mixed (a factor of 4); a minor loss with the wrong velocity.

**Unit 6. External flow**

- Boundary layers (laminar and turbulent, Blasius); drag and lift; the drag crisis.
- `boundary-layer`: a flat plate. Shows δ growing with √x in laminar flow and faster in turbulent.
  Check: the momentum-integral estimate against Blasius.
- `drag`: drag on a sphere and cylinder against Re. Shows the drag crisis. Data are a stated
  correlation, cited.
