## Units

**Unit 1. Modelling across domains**

- Mechanical translation and rotation; electrical; fluid and thermal elements; the analogies
  (effort and flow); energy methods and Lagrange's equations.
- `analogies`: a mass–spring–damper, an RLC circuit and a tank–pipe system with matched parameters.
  Shows that they produce the same response curve. Check: each simulated from its own equations.
- Application lab, `quarter-car`: a car's suspension with tyre stiffness, sprung and unsprung mass.
  Shows the two modes (body bounce and wheel hop) and how the shock trades comfort against grip.
  Typical numbers stated with a source. Check: eigen-decomposition against simulation.

**Unit 2. Laplace transforms and transfer functions**

- Transfer functions from equations; block diagrams; poles and zeros; the final-value theorem.
- `poles`: a pole–zero map with draggable poles and the step response beside it. Shows that a pole's
  real part sets the decay and its imaginary part sets the frequency. Check: inverse Laplace by partial fractions
  against RK4.

**Unit 3. Time response**

- First-order (τ); second-order (ζ, ω_n, percent overshoot, rise and settling time); higher order and
  dominant poles.
- `second-order`: ζ and ω_n sliders with the standard specs marked. Shows that overshoot depends on ζ
  only. Check: specs measured from the simulated curve against the formulas.
- Application lab, `thermometer`: a probe plunged into hot water, as a first-order lag with
  τ = mc/(hA). Shows why a reading taken too early is wrong, and why a thinner probe reads faster.
  Real probe dimensions stated with a source. What it leaves out: conduction along the stem.

**Unit 4. State space**

- State variables; state equations; conversion to and from transfer functions; simulation.
- `state-space`: a two-mass system in state form, simulated, beside its transfer function.
  Check: both routes agree.

**Unit 5. Frequency response**

- Sinusoidal steady state; Bode plots; resonance; bandwidth.
- `bode`: a second-order system's Bode plot with a sine input. Shows the gain and phase read off the
  plot matching the steady-state output. Check: simulation against the plot.

**Unit 6. Vibrations**

- Free and forced vibration of one and two degree-of-freedom systems; modes; vibration isolation;
  the tuned absorber.
- `two-dof`: two masses and three springs. Shows the two mode shapes and beats between them. Check:
  the eigen-decomposition against simulation.
- `absorber`: a tuned absorber on a forced mass. Shows the main mass standing still at the tuned
  frequency, with two new resonances either side. Check: the steady-state amplitude formula.

**Unit 7. Linearization**

- Operating points; small-signal models; validity.
- `linearize-tank`: a tank draining through an orifice (flow ∝ √h). Shows the linear model's time
  constant changing with the operating level. Check: the nonlinear simulation for small steps.
