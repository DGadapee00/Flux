## Unit 1. First-order equations

**OCW order:** conventions/prerequisites → basic DEs and separation → geometric methods → numerical
methods → first-order linear ODEs → integrating factors → complex arithmetic/exponentials →
sinusoidal functions → constant-coefficient first-order ODEs → exponential input, gain and phase
lag → autonomous equations → linear versus nonlinear → Exam 1.

Derive separation with equilibrium/domain exceptions, the integrating factor from the product
rule, Euler's step from the tangent line, complex exponential response, and stability from signs.
Existence/uniqueness means local hypotheses and maximal intervals, not “solutions never cross”
without qualifications. Non-Lipschitz examples distinguish nonuniqueness from numerical failure.

| Lab | Shows that / computation and independent check | Two predictions to seed |
|---|---|---|
| `slope-field` | The field fixes a tangent, not a straight global solution. Drag an initial point; integrate by RK4; compare separable/linear cases with closed forms. Nonunique examples display analytic branches explicitly rather than pretending RK4 finds all branches. | Does doubling the initial value always double the solution? Can two solution branches meet for y′=√\|y\| at y=0? |
| `euler` | A stable ODE can have an unstable discrete approximation. Euler steps versus exact decay and RK4; convergence ratios approach 2 only away from roundoff and singularities. | For y′=−ky, is any positive h stable? Does halving h make the global error four times smaller? |
| `phase-line` | Equilibrium stability follows arrows, even without a formula. Logistic/harvesting phase lines and RK4 trajectories; check roots and f′ signs independently. | Does increasing harvest always leave a positive equilibrium? Can a trajectory cross an equilibrium in a smooth autonomous model? |
| `first-order-response` | A sinusoidal input loses amplitude and lags; it does not merely shift vertically. Complex gain versus integrating-factor quadrature and transient RK4. | Does doubling drive frequency halve the gain at every frequency? Does an initial condition change steady-state phase? |
| `coffee` **application** | Draw a HARIO glass coffee server, coffee level, cup and temperature colours; animate cooling/pouring. Solve Newton cooling and piecewise ambient changes; compare analytic exponentials with RK4 and the energy balance. Plot temperature in a secondary panel. | Does twice the initial temperature excess take twice as long to halve? At fixed k and coffee temperature, does lowering ambient temperature leave the initial cooling rate unchanged? |

Coffee source: [HARIO XGSR-60-TB](https://global.hario.com/product/coffee/server/XGSR.html),
manufacturer dimensions **145 × 121 × 120 mm**, verified 1 Nov 2026. Use those for a recognisable
schematic silhouette, not a heat-transfer calculation from overall dimensions. Fill 300 mL,
initial/ambient temperatures and k are **chosen teaching inputs**, not HARIO retention data.
What the model leaves out: evaporation, wall temperature, variable convection, radiation and
internal gradients; fitting one k is not predicting a real server's cooling curve.

Families vary separable/linear/autonomous classification, initial value versus time versus rate
unknown, implicit/explicit/field representation, finite-time blow-up and domain, equilibrium
retention, integrating-factor IVPs, complex phasors, mixing and cooling. Named traps: neglecting
outflow uses q_in instead of q_in−q_out; dropping the ambient offset uses T0 exp(−kt) instead of
Ta+(T0−Ta) exp(−kt); dividing by y deletes y=0; ignoring absolute values loses branches.

**Assessment anchors:** Basic DE session Part I and Part II problems with solutions; Numerical
Methods Practice Problems 3 and solutions; Integrating Factors Practice Problems 5 and solutions;
Complex Arithmetic Practice Problems 6 and solutions. Exam 1 (`mit18_03scf11_ex1`) and separate
solutions (`…ex1s`); Practice Exam 1 includes solutions (`…prex1`). Select and record exact
problem identifiers during Unit 1, before writing template worked cases.

## Unit 2. Second-order linear equations

**OCW order:** modes/characteristic equation → damped oscillators → exponential response → gain
and phase lag → undetermined coefficients → linear operators/LTI → pure resonance → frequency
response/practical resonance → LRC circuits and damping ratio → Exam 2.

Derive modes by substitution; obtain the repeated-root solution as a limiting/independence
argument; derive real oscillations from complex roots. Derive forced gain/phase using complex
arithmetic and check the equation. Distinguish natural, damped and peak-response frequencies.
Critical damping's “fastest” claim must specify the initial conditions and settling criterion.

| Lab | Shows that / computation and independent check | Two predictions to seed |
|---|---|---|
| `oscillator` | Equal stiffness does not imply equal return behaviour. Animate a drawn mass, spring and dashpot; analytic root cases checked against first-order-system RK4, including the repeated-root limit. | Does more damping always settle sooner? Can a critically damped system cross equilibrium for some initial velocities? |
| `resonance` | A damped peak need not occur at the undamped natural frequency, and can disappear. Draw a driven oscillator; complex steady state plus transient RK4; compare fitted late-cycle amplitude and phase. | Is peak displacement at ω_n for every damping? Does a larger damping coefficient always reduce every transient displacement? |
| `suspension` **application** | A car body responds to road motion relative to the moving base. Draw a wheel, road, spring, shock and body; animate a bump and a washboard road. Use m z″+c(z′−r′)+k(z−r)=0; analytic smooth-segment/modal response checked against RK4 and energy balance. | Does a stiffer spring always give a smoother ride? With base excitation, is the transmitted body motion always less than road motion? |
| `rlc` | Mechanical and electrical second-order models share poles without sharing units. Draw a series RLC loop with charge/current animation; analytic charge response versus RK4/KVL residual. | Is zero current at one instant proof the capacitor is discharged? Does increasing R shift ω_n=1/√(LC)? |

Suspension source: [Eibach 0600.250.0300 ERS spring](https://eibach.com/product/0600.250.0300),
**300 lbf/in**, free length **6 in**, inside diameter **2.5 in**, verified manufacturer table
1 Nov 2026. Convert with shared constants. Sprung mass, damping and road profile are chosen
inputs; do not call them a measured vehicle or a manufacturer's shock specification.
What the model leaves out: tyre compliance, unsprung mass, nonlinear shock force, bump stops,
roll/pitch and linkage motion ratio. A one-mass quarter-car cannot predict wheel hop.

Families vary root regime, missing coefficient/initial condition, guess versus full particular
solution, resonance multiplicity, physical transfer function, amplitude/phase inference and
mechanical/electrical analogy. Traps: use ω_n for ω_d (missing √(1−ζ²)); omit the t factor at
resonance; drop the sine term from the particular solution with damping; invert base transmissibility.

**Assessment anchors:** Modes Practice Problems 9 and solutions; Damped Oscillators Practice
Problems 10 and solutions; Exponential Response Practice Problems 11 and solutions; Undetermined
Coefficients Practice Problems 13 and solutions, plus each session's posted Part I/II sets where
present. Exam 2 (`…ex2`, `…ex2s`) and Practice Exam 2 with solutions (`…prex2`).

## Unit 3. Fourier series and Laplace transforms

**OCW order:** Fourier basics → operations on Fourier series → periodic inputs/resonance → step
and delta integrals/generalized derivatives → unit step/impulse response → convolution → Laplace
basics → partial fractions/inversion → transformed IVPs → transfer/weight functions and Green's
formula → poles, amplitude response and connection to ERF → Exam 3.

Derive Fourier coefficients from orthogonality, the derivative-transform initial terms by
integration by parts, and convolution as superposition of delayed impulses. Include periodic-input
response, causal shifting, repeated poles and the distinction between zero-state transfer and
initial-condition response. Delta is an idealized distribution, not an ordinary infinite value.

| Lab | Shows that / computation and independent check | Two predictions to seed |
|---|---|---|
| `fourier` | More terms narrow a jump's ringing without eliminating its limiting overshoot. Animate harmonic phasors/wave addition; coefficients from analytic integrals checked by quadrature; Gibbs only for a jump, away from the endpoint value convention. | Does doubling N halve overshoot height? Is the series value at a jump the left-hand value? |
| `laplace` | Identical poles can have different residues and initial conditions. Animate a drawn damped mass under step/impulse forcing; inverse-transform response checked by RK4 with explicit jump conditions. | Does a transfer function alone encode nonzero initial displacement? Does a delayed input require delaying the pre-existing free motion? |
| `convolution` | Output depends on weighted input history rather than pointwise multiplication. Animate pulse contributions entering a drawn RC circuit; quadrature checked against direct ODE integration and analytic step/impulse cases. | Is output u(t)h(t)? Does a stable circuit return instantly to zero when its input stops? |
| `radio-tuner` **application** | A clock's RLC receiver favours a frequency band, while abrupt input changes ring. Draw an antenna, coil, capacitor, resistor and clock face; animate charge/current and switched carrier. Complex/Fourier and Laplace responses checked against time-domain RK4 and KVL. | Does tuning L without changing C leave the selected station fixed? Does a sharper frequency peak also mean a faster transient? |

Radio source: [NIST WWVB signal description](https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division/time-distribution/radio-station-wwvb),
**60 kHz carrier**, verified 1 Nov 2026. L, resistance and coupling are teaching inputs; C is
computed from the tuning target. Do not claim this is a particular commercial receiver circuit.
Use a carrier-resolving integration step and verify convergence; a slow envelope alone cannot
check RF phase. What the model leaves out: antenna coupling/calibration, amplifier noise,
rectification, decoding, propagation and the station's complete modulation protocol.

Families vary parity/period and coefficients, periodic forcing by harmonic response, step versus
impulse and jump conditions, delayed forcing, repeated/complex poles, convolution direction,
initial conditions and transfer functions. Traps: omit a0/2; use f(t−a) without u(t−a); reverse the
initial-value signs in L{y″}; omit a repeated-pole term; multiply time functions for convolution.

**Assessment anchors:** Fourier Practice Problems 21 and solutions; Operations 22 and solutions;
Convolution 26 and solutions; Laplace IVPs 29 and solutions; session Part I/II sets and solutions.
Exam 3 (`…ex3`, `…ex3s`); Practice Exam 3 with solutions (`mit18_03scf11_prex3_pdf`).

## Unit 4. First-order systems

**OCW order:** linear systems → matrix methods/eigenvalues/normal modes → qualitative phase
portraits → matrix exponentials → nonlinear systems → linearization near critical points →
limitations of linearization, limit cycles and chaos → cumulative final exam.

Derive the state-space conversion and eigenmode condition; explain eigenvectors geometrically.
Cover distinct/repeated/complex eigenvalues and defective matrices without dividing by the
eigenvalue gap near degeneracy. Trace, determinant and discriminant classify generic linear
portraits; repeated eigenvalues need eigenvector/rank information. Derive the Jacobian from a
first-order expansion and state the hyperbolicity condition. A linear centre cannot decide
nonlinear stability. Include qualitative limit cycles and chaos without promising closed forms.

| Lab | Shows that / computation and independent check | Two predictions to seed |
|---|---|---|
| `phase-portrait` | Eigenvectors set directions as well as eigenvalues setting growth. Animate phase particles; matrix-exponential response versus RK4 and exp(A·0)=I/residual checks. | Do equal trace/determinant imply the same eigenvectors? Do repeated eigenvalues guarantee two independent modes? |
| `normal-modes` | A coupled motion need not remain in one normal mode. Draw two coupled masses and springs, animate mode mixtures; modal solution checked against first-order-system RK4 and conservative energy. | Does moving only one mass excite just one mode? Does exchanging mode amplitude change its frequency in a linear model? |
| `linearize` | Local agreement can fail far away or at nonhyperbolic equilibria. Overlay nonlinear and linearized trajectories; Jacobian checked by finite differences and independently refined integration. Include the centre counterexample x′=−y+a x(x²+y²), y′=x+a y(x²+y²). | Is a linear centre proof of nonlinear stability? Does a locally accurate tangent model stay accurate for a full large orbit? |
| `limit-cycle` | An attracting orbit differs from an equilibrium and from a conservative centre family. Animate phase particles and return crossings for a planar nonlinear oscillator; compare step refinements and a radial/energy argument where available. | Does a stable oscillation require a stable equilibrium at its centre? Is every closed orbit an attracting limit cycle? |
| `clock-pendulum` **application** | A small-angle linear model hides amplitude-dependent timekeeping. Draw a pendulum clock, rod, bob and escapement marker; animate nonlinear angle/velocity and its linear approximation. RK4 checked against energy balance and period quadrature/`pendulum-series`. | Does a larger release angle keep exactly the same period? Does a linear centre prove an unforced damped clock keeps running forever? |

Clock source: [UK Parliament, Great Clock facts](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/big-ben/facts-figures/great-clock-facts/),
**2 s per beat**, **4.4 m overall pendulum length**. A full oscillation has two beats. Use an
**effective simple-pendulum length fitted to the 4 s small-angle period**; 4.4 m is not a measured
pivot-to-centre-of-mass length. Damping/drive are chosen inputs. What the model leaves out:
compound-pendulum mass distribution, the actual escapement and compensation, friction laws and
air effects. Do not report the teaching pendulum's error as the real clock's error.

Families vary matrix/phase portrait/trajectory representation, eigenpair matching, initial-state
mode weights, defective versus diagonalizable cases, matrix exponentials, nonlinear equilibria,
Jacobian classification and local versus global conclusions. Traps: pair an eigenvector with
the wrong eigenvalue; omit a generalized eigenvector term; classify a nonlinear centre solely
from imaginary eigenvalues; interpret trace zero as sufficient for closed trajectories.

**Assessment anchors:** Linear Systems Practice Problems 32 and solutions and exercises
`mit18_03scf11_s32_2exerq`/`…exera` (elimination), `…s32_5exerq`/`…exera` (vectors/matrices),
`…s32_7exerq`/`…exera` (matrix notation); Phase Portraits Practice Problems 34 and solutions;
Matrix Exponentials Practice Problems 35 and solutions; nonlinear session exercises and posted
answers. [Final exam page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-03sc-differential-equations-fall-2011/pages/final-exam/)
links the cumulative final, separate solutions and practice final with solutions.
